# App Archetypes

Working names are placeholders. They should preserve familiar browsing behavior without copying real platform names, marks, or exact UI.

## Loop

Short-video app.

### Content

- Vertical clips.
- Stitches, remixes, reactions, duets, captions, overlays, and comments.
- Creator-facing performance posts and casual personal clips.

### Truths It Reveals

- Tone, timing, body language, background objects, comment momentum, and who amplified a moment.
- How a narrative became viral.
- Whether a clip has been cropped, reframed, or reacted to out of order.

### Receipt Types

- Clip.
- Caption.
- Pinned comment.
- Audio reuse.
- Remix chain.
- Comment thread.
- Profile bio or recent repost.

### False Interpretations

- Cropped clips make reactions look like causes.
- Viral comments flatten nuance.
- Reaction chains separate source from amplifier.
- Old clips can look freshly posted.

### Unlocks

- Comments mention a Frame story.
- Creator profile links to Threadline.
- Caption references a Blink screenshot.
- Remix chain exposes a source account.

### Production Requirements

- Short controlled clips or still-image video placeholders.
- Realistic comments with varied voices.
- Continuity in clothing, rooms, objects, and timestamps when clips carry receipt weight.

### Risks And Adversarial Concerns

- Video continuity can become expensive.
- Performance may feel too scripted.
- Overreliance on visual micro-details can frustrate players.

## Frame

Photo/story app.

### Content

- Photo posts.
- Carousels.
- Stories.
- Close-friends-style hints.
- Profiles, tags, location stickers, and reactions.

### Truths It Reveals

- Relationship context.
- Social proximity.
- Background continuity.
- Soft-launch hints.
- Timing and event attendance.

### Receipt Types

- Story screenshot.
- Carousel image.
- Tagged photo.
- Profile highlight.
- Caption edit.
- Comment or reaction.
- Background object continuity.

### False Interpretations

- Vague posts invite projection.
- Cropped photos hide who else was present.
- Soft-launch imagery can imply the wrong timeline.
- Captions can be edited after reactions begin.

### Unlocks

- Tagged account leads to Loop.
- Story screenshot shows a Blink username.
- Caption references a Threadline thread.
- Profile highlight reveals an older context.

### Production Requirements

- Realistic fictional photos.
- Consistent outfits, rooms, props, and social group relationships.
- Story UI that feels familiar but not branded.

### Risks And Adversarial Concerns

- Too much ambiguity can feel like guessing.
- Character likeness continuity must avoid resembling real people.
- Critical proof should not depend on tiny background details.

## Threadline

Public text/discourse app.

### Content

- Short public posts.
- Replies.
- Quote-posts.
- Long threads.
- Fan commentary.
- Account bios and repost chains.

### Truths It Reveals

- Source and amplifier separation.
- Public interpretation spread.
- Timeline of claims.
- Who benefits from discourse.
- Contradictions in public statements.

### Receipt Types

- Original post.
- Quote-post chain.
- Reply screenshot.
- Deleted-post repost.
- Bio change.
- Timestamp sequence.
- Fan account thread.

### False Interpretations

- Loud accounts look like sources.
- Quote-posts strip context.
- Screenshots circulate without origin.
- Engagement incentives reward simple blame.

### Unlocks

- A post links to Loop.
- A reply references a Frame story.
- A screenshot includes a Blink contact name.
- A source account reveals an alt pattern.

### Production Requirements

- Distinct voices across public accounts.
- Clear timestamps.
- Believable public pile-on behavior.
- Finite authored thread structures.

### Risks And Adversarial Concerns

- Too much text can slow the mobile loop.
- Discourse may feel too on-the-nose.
- Public callout energy must stay fictional and contained.

## Blink

Private/ephemeral app.

### Content

- DMs.
- Temporary snaps.
- Close-circle exchanges.
- Screenshots of disappearing messages.
- Reactions and quick replies.

### Truths It Reveals

- Private context that public posts omit.
- Timing of coordination.
- Who knew what before public posting.
- Public/private mismatch.
- Soft pressure, social anxiety, and motive.

### Receipt Types

- DM exchange.
- Snap caption.
- Reaction timestamp.
- Group chat excerpt.
- Contact name.
- Ephemeral screenshot.

### False Interpretations

- Screenshots can be cropped.
- Contact names can be ambiguous.
- Private jokes collapse outside their context.
- A saved snap can look more intentional than it was.

### Unlocks

- Public comments mention "check Blink."
- A story screenshot includes a Blink handle.
- A Threadline repost leaks an ephemeral exchange.
- A Loop clip shows a notification.

### Production Requirements

- Tight, realistic private-message writing.
- Clear but minimal timestamps.
- Fictional handles and contact names.
- Private content should feel leaked or saved, not omniscient.

### Risks And Adversarial Concerns

- Private receipts can feel too convenient.
- Direct confession risk is high.
- Must avoid encouraging real-world invasive behavior; the browsing remains authored and fictional.
