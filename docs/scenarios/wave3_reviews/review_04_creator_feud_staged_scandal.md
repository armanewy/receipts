## Mystery Structure Critic

- Perspective score: 4.2 / 5
- Public interpretation plausibility: Strong. Mira's cropped line is sharp, memorable, and easy for fans to repeat.
- Actual causal chain explanatory power: Strong. Kellan's solo launch, early fan-thread timing, and old Blink context make the true story more coherent than the public betrayal read.
- Contradiction ladder strength: Good. The fan thread timestamp is a clean first wobble, Frame supplies motive, and Blink changes the quote's meaning.
- Single-receipt dependency: Moderate. The expanded Blink exchange risks solving too much if it clearly shows Kellan agreeing and the old date in one neat artifact.
- Responsible person appears early enough: Yes. Kellan is present from the opening artifact, which makes the answer fair but also raises the chance he becomes obvious.
- Wrong theories: Very strong. "Mira did it," "fan account invented it," and "both creators staged it together" each explain real pieces while failing somewhere important.
- Report brute-force risk: Moderate. Because Kellan is the opening narrator of the public story, players may try him as WHO early. The report must require WHY, WHEN, HOW, and receipts that distinguish him from the mutual-staging theory.

## Doomscroll/UX Critic

- Perspective score: 4.6 / 5
- First 30-second pull: Excellent. A silent duet over an old rehearsal clip with launch-day overlay gives instant social tension.
- App unlock momentum: Excellent. Comments drive to Threadline, Threadline drives to Frame and Blink, and each unlock answers a different question.
- One-more-thing motivation: Very strong. The player keeps asking whether the screenshot is current, why the fan account moved early, and why the solo launch was ready.
- Browsing feel: Strong. The scenario can feel like reading a public creator mess rather than interacting with a formal puzzle.
- Report screen fit: Should work if the report is framed as a theory of how the story was shaped, not as a formal judgment.
- App desire: Every app creates a fresh pull. Frame is especially useful because it turns vague-posting into motive and benefit.

## Social Realism Critic

- Perspective score: 4.1 / 5
- Posting behavior: Mostly believable. Creators do weaponize ambiguity, vague posts, fan recap accounts, and old private quotes.
- Internet-native feel: Strong. The fan account, quote chain, launch pivot, and "both creators staged it" wrong theory are all native to creator discourse.
- Receipt believability: Strong if Kellan's media-kit timing is public enough to be overlooked rather than inexplicably exposed.
- Scripted/artificial risk: Moderate. The same phrase appearing in recap and Frame can feel writerly if repeated too exactly.
- Emotional believability: Strong. Kellan's motive is not faceless or abstract: status, independence, numbers, and a cleaner solo narrative.

## Production Feasibility Critic

- Perspective score: 4.0 / 5
- Photo/video burden: Manageable. The main Loop duet, Mira rehearsal clip, and Frame rollout assets can be controlled.
- Continuity challenges: Dates across Threadline, Blink, Frame, and Loop must line up precisely and remain readable.
- Cast/location count: Low to moderate. Kellan and Mira carry the scenario, with fan accounts and commenters mostly text-only.
- Video precision: Moderate. The opening duet needs to feel like real creator content, not staged acting.
- Stills/text viability: High. Threadline chronology, Frame stories, and Blink screenshots can do much of the heavy lifting.
- IP/App Store sensitivity: Low if sponsor-like materials are fictional and do not resemble real platform or brand campaigns.

## Ethical/Safety Critic

- Perspective score: 4.0 / 5
- Real-person resemblance risk: Low with fictional cast and fictional brand context.
- Exploitative tone risk: Moderate. Public creator pile-ons can become mean-spirited if the ending reads like a call to punish Kellan.
- Harassment-like behavior: Needs careful framing. The scenario should keep the player's activity inside authored app surfaces and avoid real-world dogpile language.
- Fictional lurking boundary: Preserved if Blink is unlocked via surfaced screenshots and in-world context, not framed as invasive access.
- Ending tone: Should emphasize how the public story got shaped and why people believed it, while avoiding a revenge-post feel.

## Product Fit Critic

- Perspective score: 4.4 / 5
- North star match: Very strong. It is a mobile-first lurker scenario built from public/private mismatch, source laundering, and app-to-app context.
- Vocabulary discipline: Good. Future passes should continue using receipts, report, theory, posts, clips, comments, and app surfaces.
- Internet drama fit: Excellent. Creator feud and staged scandal sit directly inside the intended content space.
- App archetype usage: Strong. Loop supplies the hook, Threadline maps spread, Frame shows incentive, Blink reframes the quote.
- Interchangeability risk: Lower than most candidates because the collab-week/solo-launch pressure is specific and memorable.

## Top 5 Strengths

1. The opening hook is one of the strongest Wave 2 premises: fast, legible, and socially charged.
2. The public story and true story both feel plausible, which supports real uncertainty.
3. All four apps have clear semantic jobs.
4. The wrong theories are strong, especially the mutual-staging theory.
5. Production can lean on text, stills, and controlled clips without requiring fragile visual proof.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. The expanded Blink crop may carry too much explanatory weight.
2. Kellan's solo-launch assets could make him look responsible too early.
3. The fan recap account's relationship to Kellan needs more texture.
4. Phrase repetition around "cameras are on" may feel too authored if handled bluntly.
5. Mira needs a little more active presence so she is not only a harmed party in hindsight.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Keep the opening Loop duet as the start; it is the strongest part of the scenario.
- Make the fan recap account feel like a real community account with prior Kellan/Mira coverage, not just a delivery mechanism.
- Separate the old Blink date from the full quote context across two receipts or require a dependency so Blink does not solve everything at once.
- Make the solo-launch motive visible through ordinary creator behavior: waitlist reopening, archived teaser, affiliate timing, or sponsor-friendly phrasing.
- Give Mira one public action that looks evasive at first but later reads as confusion or damage control.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut any overly polished media-kit artifact that feels too conveniently public.
- Cut duplicate Frame motive receipts if they all say "solo launch was ready" in different forms.
- Cut any Blink line where Kellan directly explains why he is cropping or leaking the quote.
- Cut exact phrase repetition if it appears in too many places.
- Cut excess Threadline replies that restate the same blame without advancing source, timing, or social spread.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Clarify the exact sequence: fan teaser, cropped quote, Loop duet, Frame story, solo waitlist traffic.
- Clarify why Mira's line originally referred to a launch angle, not to Kellan as a person.
- Clarify how the player can tell the fan account is an amplifier rather than the origin.
- Clarify why Kellan benefits from canceling collab week instead of quietly moving on.
- Clarify which receipts separate Kellan-only staging from the plausible "both creators staged it together" wrong theory.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Let Kellan seem stressed and opportunistic rather than openly villainous.
- Let Mira's old wording remain a little harsh so the public reading stays tempting.
- Make the fan account's early post ambiguous enough that players wonder whether it is clout-chasing or fed a line.
- Make Frame's solo rollout look like normal creator planning until it is compared against Threadline timing.
- Keep Blink private context short and messy, not perfectly explanatory.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium. Kellan is present from the first artifact and directly benefits from the solo pivot. If Frame reveals prebuilt launch assets too early, players may settle on him before the source/timing path is satisfying.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Low to medium. The scenario has clear anchors. The main hard point is distinguishing Kellan's unilateral setup from the mutual-staging wrong theory; the receipt bundle needs to show the crop protects only him.

## Production Risk

Moderate. The timing receipts are text-friendly, but the opening duet must look convincing and the fictional solo-launch materials must feel real without invoking real brands. The crop should be readable but not too complete.

## Should Move To Wave 4?

Yes. This should move forward as a top candidate. Wave 4 should preserve the hook, deepen the fan-account source trail, split the Blink recontextualization across a bundle, and tune Kellan's motive so it emerges through browsing rather than announcing itself.
