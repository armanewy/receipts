- Verdict: REVISE
- Aggregate score: 3.93 / 5

## 1. Mystery Structure Critic

- Perspective score: 4 / 5
- Public interpretation plausibility: Strong. Nia as public face makes the first reading emotionally sticky, and the delayed refunds give the feed a simple reason to blame her.
- Actual causal chain explanatory power: Strong. Kellan's side funnel explains the refund account timing, the admin identity, and the repeated push toward the Inner Room.
- Contradiction ladder strength: Good staged progression, especially old Loop clip to Threadline timing to Blink admin details.
- Solution dependence on one receipt: Mostly avoided, though R05-10 risks becoming too decisive if it names Kellan too plainly.
- Responsible person timing: Kellan appears early enough through Frame, but his introduction should feel socially natural rather than like a new answer walking onto the screen.
- Wrong theories: Plausible and useful. The Nia theory should remain partially valid because she still owns some oversight failure.
- Brute-force risk: Moderate. Once Kellan is one of the report options, WHO may be guessable unless WHY and HOW require the larger bundle.

## 2. Doomscroll/UX Critic

- Perspective score: 4 / 5
- First 30-second pull: Strong. A calm wellness clip over angry refund comments is easy to understand immediately.
- App unlock momentum: Strong. Comments point to Threadline, Threadline points to Frame and Blink, and each move answers one question while opening another.
- One-more-thing motivation: Good. The player keeps asking who started the refund account, what the Inner Room is, and whether Nia knew.
- Browsing feel: Mostly strong. The flow reads like tapping comments, profiles, quote chains, and screenshots rather than solving formal tasks.
- Report screen risk: Low-to-moderate. The report should emphasize "this explains the source account and the rollover messages" rather than sounding like a formal judgment.
- App desire: Threadline and Blink are especially compelling. Loop needs one more meaningful surface beyond the initial hook so it does not feel disposable.

## 3. Social Realism Critic

- Perspective score: 3.8 / 5
- Posting behavior: Believable overall. Wellness creators, refund frustration, and member-led callouts are recognizable feed behavior.
- Internet-native feel: Strong. The soft-guru hypocrisy pattern is familiar without relying on a real person.
- Receipt believability: Strong for Threadline and Blink. Frame is plausible but the visible admin board risks feeling too conveniently readable.
- Scripted-feeling risk: Medium. Repeated phrase overlap may feel too neat if the wording is identical across surfaces.
- Emotional believability: Kellan's motive is plausible but needs more human pressure. The merch gap alone may read as accounting trivia unless tied to reputation, rent, debt, or a looming launch promise.

## 4. Production Feasibility Critic

- Perspective score: 3.4 / 5
- Asset burden: Manageable, with mostly text, stills, and two or three controlled clips.
- Continuity challenges: Moderate. The admin icon, Kellan's Frame reflection, Inner Room labels, timestamps, and phrase patterns need tight continuity.
- Unique people/locations: Reasonable. Nia, Kellan, Jessa, and members can be handled without a large cast.
- Video precision: Low-to-moderate. The main Loop clip can be mostly still-based or screen-record style.
- Lower-cost assets: Threadline posts, Blink messages, admin cards, and story screenshots can carry much of the scenario.
- High-risk assets: The laptop reflection and "IR rollovers" label should not be the only way to understand WHY.

## 5. Ethical/Safety Critic

- Perspective score: 4 / 5
- Real-person/event imitation: Manageable if names, visuals, and wellness branding stay fictional and avoid recognizable guru aesthetics.
- Exploitative feel: Moderate risk. Paid wellness communities can touch real vulnerability, so the tone should focus on trust, access, and social pressure rather than mocking followers.
- Harassment-like energy: Manageable. The ending should avoid cheering a pile-on and should make clear that Nia's public blame was also oversimplified.
- Fictional containment: Strong if the apps, community, and people stay clearly made-up.
- Ending safety: Needs careful language. The feed reaction should shift context, not encourage a mob against Kellan.

## 6. Product Fit Critic

- Perspective score: 4.2 / 5
- North star match: Strong. It uses browsing, receipts, app unlocks, and a structured theory/report.
- Vocabulary fit: Strong. The scenario uses receipts and social artifacts rather than barred procedural framing.
- Internet drama fit: Strong. Refunds, soft-guru branding, private community access, and source laundering are exactly in-bounds.
- Four-app use: Strongest part of the scenario. Each app reveals a different layer.
- Repeatability risk: Medium-low. The wellness setting is distinct, though the "public face blamed, helper benefits" shape should be made more specific through Kellan's personality and member dynamics.

## Top 5 Strengths

1. The surface event has immediate social pull and a clear reason to scroll comments.
2. Cross-app roles are well separated: Loop for virality, Threadline for source spread, Frame for benefit, Blink for private redirect.
3. Kellan as responsible individual explains more than the simple Nia blame story.
4. The wrong theories are plausible and socially grounded.
5. The report bundle uses timing, motive, method, and private/public mismatch rather than one oversized receipt.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. Kellan may become the obvious answer too early if Frame follows the phrase clue too directly.
2. The merch-pressure motive is currently weaker than the side-funnel method.
3. Some receipts rely on readable background details that could become production-fragile.
4. The repeated wording trail risks feeling artificial if not varied.
5. Loop risks becoming mostly a launch surface unless its old-date wobble and remix chain stay meaningful.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Make Nia imperfect in a concrete way: poor oversight, vague public language, or delayed review of admin activity. This keeps the public interpretation emotionally plausible.
- Strengthen Kellan's WHY by tying the failed merch gap to a visible deadline, reputation promise, or personal launch pressure.
- Make Kellan visible before he looks suspicious, such as through a helpful public reply or a production-credit mention.
- Split WHO support across phrase pattern, admin identity, timing, and benefit, so no single receipt over-solves.
- Give Loop a second meaningful receipt, such as a remix chain that shows source laundering or an old-date comparison.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut any version of the Frame reflection where "IR rollovers" is only legible as a tiny background detail.
- Cut exact repeated phrases across every Kellan-linked artifact; keep related phrasing without making it look mechanically matched.
- Cut any Blink message that states Kellan's full plan directly.
- Cut redundant Threadline posts if they repeat the same refund claim without changing source, timing, or amplifier context.
- Cut any follower comments that sound like player hints rather than real reactions.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Clarify what the Inner Room is before it becomes a report requirement.
- Clarify whether @QuietRefunds claims to be a member, a watcher account, or an anonymous helper.
- Clarify Nia's actual knowledge level at each point in the timeline.
- Clarify why Kellan had control over refund timing or member messages.
- Clarify how the payment note connects to personal pressure rather than generic background work.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Make Kellan's early posts helpful and bland, not suspicious.
- Let @QuietRefunds use a mix of member-like phrasing and Kellan-like phrasing, with the pattern emerging only after comparison.
- Keep Jessa's callout emotionally valid even if she is not the source of the manipulation.
- Let Nia's public calm read as ambiguous rather than obviously false.
- Make the Blink redirect feel like soft pressure, not a sales pitch written for the player to catch.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium. If R05-07 and R05-10 both point plainly to Kellan, the player can choose WHO before understanding WHY or HOW. The fix is to make the report require a stronger bundle and keep Kellan's identifiers partial until cross-app context accumulates.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Medium-low. The main hard point is source laundering: players may not naturally distinguish the loudest account from the original source unless the quote/repost chain is visually clear and scannable.

## Production Risk

Moderate. The scenario is feasible, but the visual continuity receipts should be backed by readable text alternatives. Do not make success depend on tiny labels, blurry reflections, or an icon match that can fail in generated media.

## Wave 4 Recommendation

Move to Wave 4 as a developed candidate if the next packet revises Kellan's motive, gives Loop more semantic weight, and reduces dependence on fragile visual details.
