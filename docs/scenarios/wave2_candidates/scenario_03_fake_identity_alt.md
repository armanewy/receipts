# Scenario 03: The Borrowed Hoodie

## 1. Scenario Metadata

- Title: The Borrowed Hoodie
- Category: Fake identity / alt account
- Tone: messy creator-house drama, anxious but not dark
- Estimated playtime: 25-35 minutes
- Intended difficulty: Medium
- Starting app: Loop
- Apps used: Loop, Frame, Threadline, Blink
- Primary transformation types: alt-account leakage, selective cropping, timestamp contradiction, incentive trail, source laundering, public/private mismatch
- One-sentence semantic promise: A creator is publicly blamed for running a cruel alt account, but the saved receipts show another person borrowed her look, copied her language, and timed the reveal to keep his own spot in the group.

## 2. Surface Event Semantics

- Artifact type: Loop reaction clip with a stitched screenshot of an anonymous Threadline account.
- Emotional charge: betrayal, embarrassment, pile-on energy, friend-group distrust.
- Apparent target: Leah Park, a rising editing creator in the shared studio group.
- Immediate question: Is Leah secretly running the anonymous account that has been leaking private jokes and subtweeting the group?
- Why a user keeps scrolling: The clip includes a cropped profile image that looks like Leah's green hoodie, commenters mention a private Frame story, and a tagged reply says the account knew a Blink-only nickname.

## 3. Public Interpretation Semantics

- Apparent actor: Leah Park.
- Apparent motive: jealousy after being left out of a group channel announcement.
- Apparent timeline: Leah was excluded, made the anonymous Threadline account, leaked private material, then got caught by the hoodie image and matching phrases.
- Apparent wronged party: Mara Vale, the group host whose private jokes were reposted.
- Viral supporting artifact: A Loop stitch showing the anonymous account avatar beside Leah in the same green hoodie.
- Emotional reason it spreads: It flatters a simple story: the quiet friend was secretly resentful.
- What it gets right: The anonymous account did use Leah's phrases, referenced private Blink context, and used a hoodie associated with her.
- What it gets wrong: Leah did not run the account; Noah Rusk borrowed her hoodie, copied her phrasing from edited captions, and pushed the reveal before a role change became public.

## 4. Actual Causal Chain

- Responsible individual: Noah Rusk, the group's current short-form editor.
- Motive/pressure: Leah was being invited to take over the paid edit lead role, which would push Noah out of the group's most visible work.
- Action taken: Noah created the anonymous Threadline account "roomtemp," copied Leah's caption habits, used a photo in Leah's borrowed hoodie as the avatar, and leaked small private-context posts that would make Leah look responsible.
- Manipulation/concealment method: Borrowed clothing, selective cropping of the avatar image, copied language patterns, source laundering through a fan recap account, and timed posts around Leah's visible frustration.
- True timeline: Noah created the alt account before Leah learned about the edit-lead offer; he seeded the strongest posts after a private Blink group chat mentioned the offer.
- Intended public belief: Leah was acting bitter after being excluded.
- Unintended trace left behind: Noah's export folder name appears in a Loop upload caption draft, his Frame story shows him wearing the hoodie before the avatar appeared, and his old Threadline posts share the alt's unusual punctuation and upload rhythm.
- Why this explanation is more satisfying: It explains how the account knew private context, why the posts used Leah-coded details, why the reveal landed when it did, and who benefited when Leah was removed from the edit-lead conversation.

## 5. Transformation Types Used

- Alt-account leakage: The alt repeats Noah's odd spacing around slashes, uses his late-night posting cadence, and references edit-export language Leah would not usually use.
- Selective cropping: The hoodie avatar is cropped tightly so the viewer sees Leah's signature color but not Noah's watch and edit-room chair.
- Timestamp contradiction: The alt account existed before Leah had the motive the feed gives her.
- Incentive trail: Noah gains continued edit access, channel credit, and revenue share if Leah is distrusted.
- Source laundering: A fan recap account makes the alt's claims seem wider and more verified than they are.
- Public/private mismatch: Public posts imply Leah was excluded, while Blink context shows she was being offered more responsibility.

## 6. App Semantic Roles

### Loop

- Role in scenario: Launches the pile-on through reaction clips and stitches.
- Key artifact: Mara's stitched clip comparing Leah's hoodie with the alt avatar.
- Misleading surface: The side-by-side visual seems obvious and emotionally clean.
- Truth it reveals: A caption draft references an export folder naming style Noah uses.
- Unlock trigger: Comments mention the original Threadline account and a Frame story where the hoodie appeared.
- Adversarial concern: The visual comparison cannot rely on tiny pixels; the crop needs readable continuity without becoming too easy.

### Frame

- Role in scenario: Shows social proximity, clothing movement, and role-change pressure.
- Key artifact: A story from shoot day where Noah wears Leah's hoodie while holding the edit-room badge.
- Misleading surface: Leah looks annoyed in a carousel, supporting the jealousy reading.
- Truth it reveals: Noah had access to the hoodie and knew about the edit-lead shift before the public did.
- Unlock trigger: A Frame comment references "the group chat nickname," pointing to Blink.
- Adversarial concern: Background continuity must be clear but not dependent on unreadable visual detail.

### Threadline

- Role in scenario: Hosts the alt account, repost chain, and source/amplifier split.
- Key artifact: The first "roomtemp" post and the fan recap thread that made it spread.
- Misleading surface: The alt sounds like Leah because it repeats her public caption phrases.
- Truth it reveals: The account's earliest posts use Noah's punctuation, edit terms, and posting cadence.
- Unlock trigger: A repost includes a cropped Blink screenshot with a visible group-chat name.
- Adversarial concern: Too much text could slow the scenario; Threadline receipts should be short and high-signal.

### Blink

- Role in scenario: Reveals private context without becoming a full explanation by itself.
- Key artifact: Group chat messages about the edit-lead role and Noah asking to borrow the hoodie for a shoot.
- Misleading surface: Leah's short replies can look cold when isolated.
- Truth it reveals: Leah was not excluded; Noah knew he was losing status and had physical access to the key visual detail.
- Unlock trigger: Threadline screenshot shows the group chat label and a contact alias.
- Adversarial concern: Blink must not include a direct admission; it should provide context and timing.

## 7. Progressive App Disclosure

- Starting app and why: Loop starts with the most shareable public clip and immediate judgment.
- Unlock 2 trigger: Loop comments link to the anonymous Threadline account and its first post.
- Unlock 3 trigger: Threadline repost includes a cropped Frame story thumbnail with the green hoodie.
- Unlock 4 trigger: A Frame comment and Threadline screenshot reference the Blink group chat nickname "room temp."
- What question each unlock answers: Threadline answers where the claim started; Frame answers who had access to the hoodie; Blink answers what the group privately knew and when.
- What new question each unlock creates: Threadline raises why the alt sounded edited; Frame raises why Noah had the hoodie; Blink raises why Noah posted differently after the role news.

## 9. Contradiction Ladder

- Surface belief: Leah ran the anonymous account because the hoodie and phrase match.
- First wobble: The alt existed before Leah supposedly had a reason to lash out.
- Cross-app context: Frame shows Noah wearing the hoodie before the avatar image circulated.
- Timeline break: Blink shows Noah heard the edit-lead news before the alt escalated, while Leah did not.
- Source/method link: Threadline language patterns and Loop export naming connect Noah to the alt's voice and handle.
- Motive/benefit: Leah's removal protects Noah's edit role and social status in the group.
- Confirmation bundle: R03-03, R03-04, R03-05, R03-08, R03-09, and R03-10 together support WHO, WHY, WHEN, and HOW.

## 10. Report Requirements

- Correct WHO: Noah Rusk.
- Correct WHY: He wanted to keep the paid edit-lead role and avoid being replaced by Leah.
- Correct WHEN: The alt began before Leah's public frustration and escalated after Noah learned about the role change.
- Correct HOW: He copied Leah-coded language, borrowed her hoodie for a cropped avatar, and let fan recap posts amplify the claim.
- Minimum required receipts: R03-03, R03-04, R03-05, R03-08, R03-09, R03-10.
- Optional supporting receipts: R03-02, R03-07, R03-11.
- Accepted substitute receipt bundles: R03-05 plus R03-08 may cover hoodie access; R03-03 plus R03-04 plus R03-10 may cover account linkage if one language receipt is missed.

## 11. Plausible Wrong Theories

### Leah ran the alt because she was jealous

- Why plausible: Hoodie, phrase match, and annoyed Frame carousel all point toward her.
- What it explains: The public clip and why comments reacted quickly.
- What it fails to explain: The alt's early timestamp, Noah's hoodie access, and the export-folder link.
- Social-native feedback: "This explains the hoodie read, but not why the account was posting before Leah knew what changed."

### Mara staged the reveal for sympathy

- Why plausible: Mara benefits from attention and posted the viral Loop clip.
- What it explains: Why the reveal became polished and shareable.
- What it fails to explain: The alt's Noah-coded language and Noah's role pressure.
- Social-native feedback: "Mara amplified the story, but the source trail starts somewhere else."

### A random fan account invented everything

- Why plausible: The fan recap account escalates the story and sounds overconfident.
- What it explains: Source laundering and public pile-on.
- What it fails to explain: Private hoodie access and Blink role timing.
- Social-native feedback: "The recap made it travel, but it did not create the private details."

## 12. Ending Semantics

- Public story: Leah was exposed as the bitter friend behind the anonymous account.
- True story: Noah created the alt to make Leah look untrustworthy before she could replace him as edit lead.
- Emotional payoff: The player gets the satisfaction of re-reading the hoodie, phrase, and timing as a planted image rather than a simple gotcha.
- Why the false story spread: It was visually simple, emotionally sticky, and repeated by a recap account that looked independent.
- How the feed reacts after the report: Comments split between apologizing to Leah, noticing Noah's old posts, and arguing that the group still ignored warning signs.

## 14. Production Feasibility Notes

- Realistic photos needed: 4-6 stills for Frame, including hoodie continuity, group lunch, edit-room setup.
- Short videos needed: 2 Loop clips, both controllable through still-heavy vertical video.
- Comments/text content needed: 20-30 comments/posts across Loop and Threadline, plus 2 short Blink snippets.
- Risky assets: Hoodie/avatar comparison if it depends on too-small details; avoid face matching as the key link.
- Assets that can be made from stills: Loop reaction clip, Frame stories, carousel, Threadline screenshots.
- Assets that require careful continuity: Green hoodie, Noah's watch, edit-room chair, timestamps around the role-change messages.

## 15. Self-Adversarial Critique

- What is too obvious: Noah wearing the hoodie could point too quickly if it appears before the alt timestamp wobble.
- What is too hidden: Punctuation and export-folder naming may feel too subtle unless paired with clearer timing and motive receipts.
- What app feels unnecessary: None, though Loop and Threadline overlap on spread mechanics; Loop should carry emotion while Threadline carries source timing.
- What receipt feels too confession-like: R03-10 risks feeling too convenient if the file name is too perfect; it should look like a messy export habit, not a label saying what happened.
- Where players might get stuck: They may over-trust the hoodie side-by-side and ignore early timestamps.
- What feels unrealistic: A fan recap account moving instantly may need a believable micro-community around the group.
- What should be changed before playtesting: Make Leah's apparent motive stronger without making Noah invisible, and tune R03-10 so it supports rather than solves the report alone.
