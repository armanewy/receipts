# Full Content Adversarial Review

## Scope Reviewed

This review covers the Wave 9 full-content plan for The Empty Fifth Chair, with emphasis on report options, endings, receipt coverage, and readiness for prototype data modeling.

## Too Obvious?

Moderate risk.

Mina owns the opening Frame story, the highlight rename, Cedar Room benefit, and source-path supports. Players may guess Mina early if all of those surfaces appear with too much polish or if her captions sound too pointed.

Mitigations:

- Keep Mina emotionally plausible in early Frame.
- Let the public Rhea read feel socially sticky through S02-R01, S02-R04, and S02-R06.
- Delay Cedar Room meaning until after Blink introduces Thursday pressure.
- Make S02-R11 work as a bundle, not as a single "Mina did it" moment.

## Too Hidden?

Moderate risk.

The correct report depends on players connecting early Frame curation, the Tuesday Loop clip, the Threadline source path, uncropped Blink context, and Cedar Room benefit. If any surface is too subtle, players may land on @porchwatch or Dax as satisfying partial answers.

Mitigations:

- Make S02-R02 timing mobile-readable.
- Give S02-R07 enough specificity to imply "inside Mina's circle" without naming Mina.
- Make S02-R10 clearly connect Rhea, Mina, Thursday, and shared credit.
- Keep S02-R12 visually distinct so Cedar Room registers as pressure, not random atmosphere.

## Too Much Text?

Potentially, especially in Threadline and Blink.

Threadline should stay short and scannable. Blink should feel like private fragments, not a transcript written for the player. The report options can be text-heavy in docs, but the playable version should surface shorter feedback states.

Recommended approach:

- Use short posts and replies in Threadline.
- Keep Blink artifacts to 4-6 tight message excerpts.
- Use receipt cards to summarize apparent and actual meaning outside the in-world content.

## Too Much Content?

The 15-receipt graph is feasible for a 30-40 minute MVP, but the prototype should distinguish critical receipts from atmospheric texture. If every item feels equally important, the player may save everything without forming a theory.

Risk areas:

- Optional S02-R03, S02-R08, S02-R13, S02-R14, and S02-R15 may become noisy if presented with the same weight as required receipts.
- The group-world warmth can become too long before the first contradiction.

Recommended cuts if pacing drags:

- Reduce atmospheric Frame comments.
- Keep one old Supper Five warmth card instead of several.
- Make S02-R14 ending texture rather than a required mid-path stop.

## Not Enough Content?

The first slice is strong, but the middle needs enough browsing texture to avoid feeling like a straight path from one required artifact to the next.

Likely gaps:

- @porchwatch needs at least one normal non-critical post or profile surface so the account does not exist only to carry S02-R06.
- Dax needs enough casual presence to make his clip feel socially natural.
- Rhea needs one imperfect public/private beat so she does not become too clean.

## Confusing App Unlock?

The app order remains strong:

- Frame points to Loop through Dax's table clip.
- Loop points to Threadline through @porchwatch references.
- Threadline points to Blink through the group-chat name and crop questions.
- Blink points back to Frame through Thursday and Cedar Room.

The main risk is overusing comments as unlock triggers. The final content should vary unlock cues with repost stickers, tagged handles, profile links, group names, and screenshot labels.

## Report Brute-Force Risk?

Moderate.

Because Mina is a WHO option and appears in the opening, some players may try Mina early. The report design reduces this by requiring WHY, WHEN, HOW, and receipts together.

Required protections:

- WHO alone never succeeds.
- S02-R09 and S02-R11 must both be needed for the crop-source path.
- S02-R10 and S02-R12 must be needed for motive.
- S02-R02 and S02-R04 must anchor the timeline.
- Feedback should identify missing context categories rather than reveal the right choice.

## Unrealistic Social Behavior?

Mostly believable, with two pressure points.

Believable elements:

- Friend-group curation through Frame.
- A small Threadline account carrying a cropped screenshot.
- A short Loop clip overriding more complicated context.
- Quiet friends avoiding public correction because of access pressure.

Risk areas:

- Mina cannot feel like she planned every beat with perfect control.
- Nia and Sol's silence should feel uncomfortable and human, not like they are protecting a twist.
- @porchwatch should sound like a small gossip/event account, not an all-knowing narrator.

## Production Difficulty?

Feasible for MVP.

Hardest assets:

- S02-R01 empty-chair story that reads as natural.
- S02-R04 Loop table clip or still-frame sequence.
- S02-R09 uncropped Blink chat with clear but not over-explained context.
- S02-R11 crop-source alignment bundle without tiny visual dependency.
- S02-R12 Cedar Room announcement that feels like pressure without becoming the main actor.

Easiest assets:

- Threadline posts and replies.
- Frame profile/highlight mocks.
- Text-only report feedback.
- Blink side DMs.
- Cedar Room profile copy.

## Exact Suggested Cuts And Revisions

1. Cut any first-slice comment that directly says the player should open Loop.
2. Use one softer Mina caption as default: "some seats stay empty for a reason."
3. Keep Cedar Room likes or profile hints hidden until after Blink unless playtests show players need earlier pressure.
4. Reduce repeated "table" metaphors in Mina's public copy so S02-R08 stays suggestive, not obvious.
5. Limit @porchwatch to short posts; avoid long explanation threads.
6. Make Dax's caption careless, not strategic.
7. Keep Rhea's private tone direct and imperfect; avoid making her sound wholly blameless.
8. Make Nia's side DM optional, not central to the correct report.
9. Do not let S02-R11 appear before S02-R09; the crop-source bundle needs the uncropped chat first.
10. Keep ending reactions small and mixed; avoid a triumphant public reversal.

## Readiness For Prototype Data Modeling

Ready for prototype data modeling: yes.

The receipt IDs, report slots, required bundles, partial outcomes, and ending states are defined enough to translate into structured content data. The main remaining work is content QA and tuning, not scenario structure.
