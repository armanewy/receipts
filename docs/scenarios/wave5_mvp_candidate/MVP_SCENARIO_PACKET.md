# MVP Scenario Packet: The Empty Fifth Chair

## 1. Title

The Empty Fifth Chair.

## 2. Category

Friend-group betrayal.

## 3. Tone

Public friend-group fallout, soft social exclusion, status anxiety, and private context that changes how the feed reads the same artifacts.

## 4. Estimated Playtime

30-40 minutes.

## 5. Cast

| Character / account | Role |
|---|---|
| Mina Saye | Public host of the birthday dinner and the responsible individual shaping the story. |
| Rhea Vale | Missing fifth friend; publicly framed as disloyal, privately asking for credit and context. |
| Dax Moreno | Friend whose Loop table clip makes the public story feel emotionally true. |
| Nia Bell | Friend who stays quiet because she only saw partial context and wants continued event access. |
| Sol Park | Friend who receives Nia's side DM and helps make the group silence believable. |
| Tessa Row | Dinner friend used mostly for atmosphere and group texture. |
| @porchwatch | Small Threadline account that carries the cropped Blink screenshot. |
| Cedar Room | Fictional supper venue/opportunity account that creates status pressure and benefit context. |

## 6. Surface Event

The player opens Frame and sees Mina's birthday dinner story: four friends at a table, a visibly empty fifth chair, and a pointed caption about screenshots. The post implies Rhea was removed from the group after leaking private Blink chat material.

The hook works because the image is simple, emotional, and shareable: a friend has been cut out, and the feed already seems to know why.

## 7. Public Interpretation

The feed believes Rhea leaked a private Blink chat because she was jealous or resentful about being pushed out of Mina's birthday dinner and the supper-club circle.

The public story is supported by:

- The empty fifth chair.
- A Loop clip of Rhea leaving during Mina's toast.
- A cropped Blink screenshot posted by @porchwatch.
- Comments framing Rhea as a screenshotter.
- The renamed "Supper Four" highlight.

What it gets right: the group really did fracture, a private chat crop really did travel publicly, and Rhea had conflict with Mina.

What it gets wrong: Mina prepared the public cut before the screenshot spread, the crop removed her own lines, the table clip was out of order, and Mina had a status reason to make Rhea look unreliable.

## 8. Actual Causal Chain

Mina wanted a solo Cedar Room supper-series opportunity while avoiding questions about whether the concept came from the group's shared "Supper Five" planning.

Rhea privately asked Mina to credit the group before Thursday. Mina then:

- Renamed the public group highlight from "Supper Five" to "Supper Four."
- Cropped a Blink group chat from her own view.
- Let @porchwatch carry the crop so it looked audience-discovered.
- Allowed Dax's dinner clip to circulate out of order.
- Used the empty-chair image to make the social cut look like a response rather than a setup.

The true chain explains why the Frame cut was early, why the Loop clip date matters, why the Blink crop changes meaning when expanded, why the group stayed quiet, and why Cedar Room mattered.

## 9. Transformation Types

- Selective cropping: the Blink screenshot hides Mina's removed lines and Rhea's de-escalation.
- Source laundering: @porchwatch makes Mina's crop feel like outside gossip.
- Temporal inversion: the Loop dinner clip is framed as a reaction to the public leak, but it happened first.
- Public/private mismatch: Mina's public loyalty language conflicts with her private handling of the shared concept.
- Role reversal: Rhea appears disloyal, but the full context shows she was asking to keep the conflict private and credit the group.
- Incentive trail: Cedar Room gives Mina a social reason to reshape the story before Thursday.
- Amplifier/source separation: Dax and @porchwatch spread the story, but neither explains who shaped the original crop.
- Context collapse: private project-credit tension becomes public friendship judgment.

## 10. App Roles

| App | Role |
|---|---|
| Frame | Public curation, group status, old warmth, highlight changes, Cedar Room benefit. |
| Loop | Emotional table clip, out-of-order repost, comment friction around timing. |
| Threadline | Cropped screenshot spread, source-chain ambiguity, public interpretation hardening. |
| Blink | Private context, uncropped chat, Rhea/Mina credit pressure, group-silence nuance. |

## 11. App Unlock Sequence

1. Start on Frame with the empty-chair story.
2. Frame unlocks Loop through Mina's repost sticker and comments about Dax's table clip.
3. Loop unlocks Threadline through comments naming @porchwatch's screenshot.
4. Threadline unlocks Blink through the exposed group-chat name and crop questions.
5. Blink sends the player back to Frame for Cedar Room context.
6. Returning to Threadline shows the public story wobbling after timing/source questions spread.

## 13. Report Structure

Correct report:

- WHO: Mina Saye.
- WHY: She wanted Rhea discredited before the Cedar Room announcement exposed the shared supper-club idea.
- WHEN: Mina reshaped the public group image Wednesday morning before the @porchwatch crop spread; the Loop clip was from Tuesday.
- HOW: Mina cropped a Blink chat from her view, let @porchwatch carry it, and used Frame curation plus out-of-order Loop context to make Rhea look disloyal.
- RECEIPTS: S02-R01, S02-R02, S02-R04, S02-R06, S02-R07, S02-R09, S02-R10, S02-R11, S02-R12.

## 14. Ending Semantics

The public story says Rhea leaked private messages and got cut out.

The true story says Mina shaped the public frame before the feed had context, using a cropped private chat and out-of-order dinner clip to protect her solo status opportunity.

The emotional payoff should be social recontextualization: the chair, clip, screenshot, and announcement all mean something different after the report. The feed reaction should show the story softening and splintering, not a revenge pile-on.

## 15. Why This Scenario Fits The Product

The Empty Fifth Chair fits Receipts because it is browsing-first, cross-app, mobile-readable, and built from social artifacts. The player is pulled by a feed image, follows app-to-app social references, saves receipts, compares context, and builds a theory that explains more than the loudest public version.
