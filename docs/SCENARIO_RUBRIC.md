# Scenario Rubric

Score each category from 1 to 5.

## Surface Event Pull

- 1: Confusing, low-stakes, or emotionally flat.
- 2: Understandable but not scroll-worthy.
- 3: Clear enough to start browsing.
- 4: Strong social hook with immediate curiosity.
- 5: Instantly legible, emotionally sticky, and demands one more tap.

## Public Interpretation Plausibility

- 1: Nobody would believe it.
- 2: Requires too much contrivance.
- 3: Plausible in a narrow context.
- 4: Believable and socially contagious.
- 5: Feels like exactly the story the feed would amplify.

## True Causal Chain Satisfaction

- 1: Less interesting than the surface story.
- 2: Explains only a few artifacts.
- 3: Coherent but expected.
- 4: Reframes many receipts cleanly.
- 5: More emotionally and structurally satisfying than the public interpretation.

## Receipt Quality

- 1: Receipts are vague, arbitrary, or confession-like.
- 2: Too few receipts matter.
- 3: Functional receipt set.
- 4: Strong apparent and actual meanings.
- 5: Graph-like receipt system with dependencies, substitutions, and recontextualization.

## Cross-App Necessity

- 1: Could happen on one app with no loss.
- 2: Other apps feel decorative.
- 3: At least two apps matter.
- 4: Most apps reveal distinct truths.
- 5: Every app surface has a necessary semantic role.

## Contradiction Ladder Strength

- 1: No meaningful progression.
- 2: One large twist.
- 3: Basic staged doubt.
- 4: Strong escalation from wobble to confirmation.
- 5: Every stage motivates the next action and changes prior meaning.

## Report Clarity

- 1: WHO, WHY, WHEN, HOW are unclear.
- 2: Report answer feels arbitrary.
- 3: Correct answer is coherent.
- 4: Slots map well to receipt bundles.
- 5: Report feels like the natural expression of the player's theory.

## Wrong Theory Plausibility

- 1: Wrong options are obviously fake.
- 2: Wrong options are arbitrary.
- 3: Some plausible partial readings.
- 4: Strong true-but-incomplete alternatives.
- 5: Wrong theories teach missing context and make the final theory stronger.

## Doomscroll Momentum

- 1: No reason to continue.
- 2: Occasional interest.
- 3: Some one-more-tap motivation.
- 4: Strong sequence of curiosity, doubt, and confirmation.
- 5: The player constantly wants one more receipt.

## Realism

- 1: People would not behave this way.
- 2: Social behavior feels scripted.
- 3: Acceptably plausible.
- 4: Internet-native and emotionally believable.
- 5: Feels like a fictional but recognizable feed event.

## Non-Game Feel

- 1: Feels like a puzzle board.
- 2: Heavy UI/game framing.
- 3: Some browsing feel.
- 4: Mostly social-native.
- 5: The experience feels like browsing first, structured theory second.

## Production Feasibility

- 1: Requires unrealistic media volume or precision.
- 2: Too many unique assets or fragile continuity details.
- 3: Feasible with constraints.
- 4: Mostly achievable with text, stills, and controlled clips.
- 5: High impact with manageable assets and low visual-risk dependencies.

## Ethical Safety

- 1: High risk of real-person resemblance, harassment energy, or exploitative framing.
- 2: Several unresolved safety concerns.
- 3: Manageable with review.
- 4: Fictional, contained, and clearly product-safe.
- 5: Strong safety posture while preserving drama.

## Adversarial Robustness

- 1: Falls apart under critique.
- 2: Many obvious weaknesses.
- 3: Some fixable issues.
- 4: Handles most critique.
- 5: Strong across clarity, realism, production, and product fit.

## Minimum Acceptable Score

A scenario should average at least 3.5, with no score below 3 in surface event pull, true causal chain satisfaction, receipt quality, cross-app necessity, report clarity, realism, or ethical safety.

## Automatic Rejection Criteria

- Uses detective, police, case-file, suspect, culprit, perp, investigation, or crime-scene language as product-facing framing.
- Centers the final answer on a faceless institution rather than an individual without explicit justification.
- Depends on direct confession as the only meaningful proof.
- Requires real people, public figures, lookalikes, real app branding, or exact real-platform UI.
- Is horror, supernatural, or crime-first when not explicitly requested.
- Can be solved from one receipt with no meaningful graph.
- Cannot be produced without fragile tiny-pixel proof.

## Too Obvious

A scenario is too obvious when the public interpretation collapses immediately, the responsible person is the only named person with motive, the first contradiction gives away WHO and HOW, or wrong theories cannot survive even casual browsing.

## Too Obscure

A scenario is too obscure when the true path depends on hidden timestamps, nearly invisible background details, unfamiliar platform behavior, or receipt dependencies that players cannot reasonably infer.

## Too Gamey

A scenario is too gamey when content exists only to serve slots, app unlocks feel like levels, comments sound like hints, or the report feels disconnected from browsing behavior.

## Too Passive

A scenario is too passive when the player only reads a linear feed, receives answers without comparing context, or has no meaningful reason to save, revisit, and assemble receipts.
