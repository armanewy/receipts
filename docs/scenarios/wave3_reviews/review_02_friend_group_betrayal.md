## 1. Mystery Structure Critic

The public interpretation is highly plausible: Rhea is framed as the private-chat leaker, and the empty chair gives the feed an easy symbol. The actual causal chain is more explanatory because Mina's timeline accounts for the early highlight change, the cropped Blink view, the out-of-order dinner clip, and the Cedar Room benefit.

The contradiction ladder is strong. It begins with a public exclusion image, then reveals that the social cut happened before the public leak, then uses the Loop clip date and Blink crop to show how the story was shaped. The solution does not depend on a single receipt. S02-R11 is important, but the report can be supported by timing, language pattern, uncropped chat, and motive receipts.

The responsible person appears early enough because Mina is central to the first artifact, but she does not become the only possible answer immediately. Wrong theories are plausible: Rhea as jealous leaker, @porchwatch as the main driver, the whole group as coordinated, or Dax as the clip amplifier. Brute-force risk is moderate only if WHO choices are too few; the report should require a supported WHY and HOW to prevent guessing.

Perspective score: 4.6 / 5

## 2. Doomscroll/UX Critic

The first 30-second pull is strong but slightly quieter than the breakup scenario because the emotional charge depends on understanding the friend group. A few warm pre-fracture thumbnails or comments can make the empty chair sting faster.

App unlock momentum is excellent. Frame creates the social exclusion hook, Loop makes the moment feel embodied, Threadline reveals what everyone is reacting to, and Blink changes the meaning of the private-chat screenshot. The player has a clear "one more thing" motivation at each step: what did Rhea screenshot, when did the dinner happen, who had the crop, and why did Mina need the story to travel?

The experience mostly feels like browsing. The main UX risk is S02-R11, where chat color/admin pin placement might become too puzzle-like. It should be paired with social context and repeated layout cues so it reads as a recognizable screenshot source, not a tiny UI challenge.

Perspective score: 4.3 / 5

## 3. Social Realism Critic

The scenario feels internet-native. Friend groups curating public status, private chat screenshots becoming public, vague loyalty captions, and a small gossip account carrying a cropped artifact are all believable. Mina's motive is emotionally plausible: she wants credit and opportunity while avoiding a direct fight about ownership.

The behavior that needs more support is the remaining friends' silence. If they all know partial context, why do they let Mina shape the public story? The scenario can answer this with social pressure, event access, fear of being next, or not realizing the crop's full meaning at first.

Receipts fit their surfaces well. Frame is ideal for public curation, Loop for embodied awkwardness, Threadline for source spread, and Blink for private context. The true theory is believable because it is selfish and status-driven without becoming extreme.

Perspective score: 4.2 / 5

## 4. Production Feasibility Critic

This is one of the most feasible candidates. It can rely heavily on Frame stills, Threadline posts, Blink chats, and a single controlled Loop clip. The empty chair motif is visually clear and does not require complex action.

Continuity challenges include the dinner date order, group names, Mina's recurring phrases, the supper-club concept language, and the cropped/uncropped Blink view. These are manageable if the content bible defines exact timestamps, handle names, group chat labels, and phrase variants before final content writing.

The hardest asset is the Loop dinner clip because bad acting could make it feel staged. The easiest assets are the public text chain, highlight screenshots, and private chat excerpts. App Store/IP sensitivity is low if Cedar Room and all app surfaces remain fictional and no exact real-platform UI is copied.

Perspective score: 4.4 / 5

## 5. Ethical/Safety Critic

The scenario is fictional and contained, with no real people, public figures, or real app branding. The central drama is social exclusion and reputation shaping, which fits the product while staying away from heavier harm.

The key safety concern is that the premise includes public pile-on dynamics. The ending should avoid making the player feel like they are launching a callout. It should instead emphasize a clearer, quieter read of the timeline and how the public story over-simplified Rhea.

Blink content must feel like authored context surfaced inside the product, not an invitation to seek private messages in real life. The use of group-chat screenshots is acceptable if the game framing keeps it fictional and finite.

Perspective score: 4.1 / 5

## 6. Product Fit Critic

This scenario strongly matches the north star. It is a lurker-first social browsing experience with receipts, app unlocks, wrong theories, and a report built from WHO, WHY, WHEN, HOW, and receipts. The current draft avoids restricted product-facing vocabulary and stays in internet drama rather than procedural framing.

All four app archetypes matter. The scenario is also repeatable as a type without feeling generic because the empty fifth chair, supper-club pitch, and cropped private chat give it specific social texture. It should move forward as a top Wave 4 candidate.

Perspective score: 4.5 / 5

## Top 5 Strengths

1. The empty chair is a simple, memorable first image with immediate social meaning.
2. The receipt graph is strong and genuinely cross-app, with timing, crop, source, and motive all linked.
3. Mina's responsible role is emotionally believable and specific.
4. The wrong theories are true-but-incomplete rather than arbitrary.
5. Production is practical because text/stills can carry most of the scenario.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. S02-R11 risks feeling like a UI-detail puzzle if it depends on chat color or pin placement alone.
2. The group's silence after Mina shapes the story needs stronger social logic.
3. The Cedar Room benefit may identify Mina too early if revealed before enough ambiguity builds.
4. Loop may feel decorative unless the dinner clip timing becomes a meaningful emotional turn.
5. Mina's repeated phrases could feel too neat if @porchwatch echoes them too closely.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Add warm group-history content before or near the empty-chair post so the exclusion has emotional weight.
- Make the remaining friends' silence plausible through opportunity pressure, incomplete knowledge, or fear of losing access.
- Pair the Blink source-view detail with stronger non-visual support, such as who had admin access or who used the same crop boundary elsewhere.
- Delay or soften the Cedar Room benefit so it becomes motive confirmation rather than an early answer.
- Give Loop a stronger role by making the dinner clip emotionally misleading and temporally important, not just another artifact.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut any repeated Mina phrase that exactly matches @porchwatch word-for-word.
- Cut any Blink line that states Mina's full motive directly.
- Cut any comment that tells players the timeline answer outright.
- Cut any asset dependency on small UI decorations that cannot be read comfortably on mobile.
- Cut duplicate group-history posts if they do not sharpen the empty-chair emotional context.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Make the Frame highlight rename time and Threadline leak time easy to compare.
- Clarify that the Loop dinner clip happened before the public leak without relying on background props.
- Show what the supper-club concept is in simple, social-native terms.
- Make the cropped versus uncropped Blink chat difference understandable at a glance.
- Make report feedback explain missing context in social language, especially if a player blames Rhea or @porchwatch.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Let Mina's motive show through small status signals and opportunity language instead of obvious self-interest.
- Make @porchwatch look like a plausible gossip account, not a direct extension of Mina.
- Give Rhea some imperfect behavior so she does not become too clean.
- Let other friends react with mixed uncertainty rather than unified silence.
- Keep the empty-chair caption sharp but not theatrical.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium. Mina is central from the first post and the Cedar Room announcement can point strongly to her. The scenario should keep early Mina readings plausible but incomplete until the player combines the pre-leak highlight change, crop source, private pitch DM, and benefit.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Medium. The crop-source logic could become obscure if players do not understand why the screenshot view matters. The fix is to support that conclusion with multiple social receipts, not just UI layout.

## Production Risk

Low to moderate. The scenario has a manageable cast, one essential clip, strong still-image surfaces, and mostly text-based receipts. The main production risk is making the dinner clip and private chat screenshots feel natural rather than staged or overly designed.
