# Wave 3 Review: Scenario 07 - The Quiet Boost

## Overall Verdict

- Verdict: REVISE
- Overall numeric score: 4.00 / 5.00
- Rubric total: 56 / 70
- Should move to Wave 4: Yes, if the analytics and admin-access mechanics are simplified before deeper development.

The scenario has a strong product fit: it turns a broad platform-adjacent grievance into a personal reach-panic story with a specific responsible individual, a concrete benefit, and a cross-app receipt bundle. Its main risk is clarity. The player must understand upload visibility, source/amplifier separation, dashboard access, and product-launch timing without the experience feeling technical or like a puzzle layer placed over a feed.

## Critic Perspectives

### 1. Mystery Structure Critic

- Score: 4.0 / 5
- Public interpretation plausibility: Strong. A small creator being buried after rejecting a collective offer is emotionally sticky and easy for the feed to repeat.
- Actual causal chain explanatory power: Strong. Mina's admin access and competing mini-drop explain the reach drop, the leak timing, and the benefit better than a vague platform story.
- Contradiction ladder strength: Good, especially the early Threadline timestamp and later Blink scheduling context.
- Single-receipt dependency: Moderate risk. PLAT-10 currently carries a lot of HOW; it should be supported more clearly by PLAT-03 and PLAT-04.
- Responsible person visibility: Mina appears early enough through Frame and Blink, but she needs a believable supportive public role before the player starts considering her.
- Wrong theories: Strong. Beck is especially useful because the public interpretation gets some real context right.
- Brute-force risk: Moderate. If Mina is the only person with dashboard access and a visible competing drop, players may choose her before understanding HOW.

### 2. Doomscroll/UX Critic

- Score: 4.0 / 5
- First 30-second pull: Strong. A creator showing a flat-lined graph over a launch panic is an immediate scroll-stopper.
- App unlock momentum: Strong in concept. Loop to Threadline is especially natural because comments would point to the rumor source.
- "One more thing" motivation: Good. Each unlock creates a sharper source question.
- Browsing versus puzzle feel: Some risk. Upload history and visibility states can feel like system mechanics unless presented as ordinary creator-dashboard screenshots.
- Report screen fit: Likely clean if the report language stays social-native: "who shaped the launch window" rather than technical platform wording.
- App desire: Threadline and Frame create strong reasons to continue; Blink must be teased earlier so it does not feel like a late answer container.

### 3. Social Realism Critic

- Score: 3.8 / 5
- Would people post this way: Mostly yes. Creators post launch panic, fans pile on, rumor accounts launder screenshots, and friends post supportive prep content.
- Internet-native drama: Strong. The public story uses familiar reach anxiety without making the platform itself the central answer.
- Receipt believability: Good, though the upload-version artifact needs to feel like something a creator would actually expose or that a viewer could naturally notice.
- Scripted/artificial risk: Mina's phrase reuse and similar mini-drop could feel too conveniently aligned if both appear close together.
- Emotional believability: Solid. Career pressure and adjacent product overlap give Mina a human motive.

### 4. Production Feasibility Critic

- Score: 3.6 / 5
- Photo/video asset burden: Manageable: two controlled clips, several stills, and text-heavy public discourse.
- Continuity challenges: Medium. The bag, timestamps, launch status, and Mina's sample shoot all need consistent ordering.
- Unique people/locations: Reasonable.
- Video precision: Low to medium. The main Loop clip can be talking-head plus overlay, but it must feel convincing.
- Text/stills potential: Strong. Threadline, Frame, and Blink can carry much of the story.
- IP sensitivity: Analytics and dashboard UI must stay fictional and should avoid copying real platform layouts.

### 5. Ethical/Safety Critic

- Score: 4.2 / 5
- Real-person/event risk: Low if names, visuals, and creator niche stay fictional.
- Exploitative feel: Manageable. The drama is about launch manipulation and status pressure, not real-world harm.
- Harassment-like behavior risk: Moderate if the ending encourages a pile-on against Mina. Keep the post-report feed restrained and centered on changed access and corrected context.
- Fictional contained lurking: Strong. The player is browsing authored surfaces and saving receipts.
- Ending safety: The proposed restrained Juno update is a good direction.

### 6. Product Fit Critic

- Score: 4.2 / 5
- North star match: Strong. It feels like feed browsing with authored payoff.
- Vocabulary fit: The scenario generally uses receipts, theory/report language, and social artifacts. Continue avoiding professional-procedure framing in future packets.
- Internet drama fit: Strong. Creator panic, rumor amplification, and status-adjacent launch timing fit the product well.
- Four-app use: Strongest part of the scenario; every app has a role.
- Repeatability risk: Medium. Platform-adjacent stories can become interchangeable if every beat is "chart, rumor, private chat." The product drop and Mina's styling account give this one useful specificity.

## Top 5 Strengths

1. The surface event is timely, emotionally sticky, and immediately understandable.
2. The true story centers on an individual with access, motive, and a clear benefit.
3. Cross-app structure is meaningful: Loop shows panic, Threadline shows spread, Frame shows access/benefit, Blink shows timing.
4. Wrong theories are true-but-incomplete and should help players learn the receipt graph.
5. The receipt bundle supports WHO, WHY, WHEN, and HOW without relying on a single direct reveal.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. Upload visibility and dashboard mechanics may be too technical for a social browsing fantasy.
2. Mina's sample-bag story and mini-drop teaser could make her too obvious too soon.
3. PLAT-10 risks becoming the one receipt players treat as the answer unless its meaning stays soft.
4. Threadline source/amplifier logic could become text-heavy and slow the mobile loop.
5. The scenario needs clearer boundaries around how Mina had access without making Juno seem implausibly careless.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Make Mina's supportive role visible before the sample-bag receipt becomes suspicious. She should first read as helpful, competent, and close to Juno.
- Replace technical upload terminology with plain fictional UI labels that a casual player can parse quickly.
- Keep Beck's pressure real and socially messy so the public interpretation remains plausible through the midpoint.
- Make the analytics screenshot useful but incomplete; do not let the chart alone imply the answer.
- Spread HOW across at least three receipts: upload state, early rumor timestamp, and Mina's scheduling advice.
- Give Mina's motive a softer emotional pressure, such as fear her own drop will disappear under Juno's launch, rather than pure sabotage energy.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut or delay PLAT-06 if the repeated phrase "quiet boost" makes Mina feel tagged by the writer.
- Cut excess chart commentary in Threadline; one source post, a short quote chain, and one correction reply may be enough.
- Cut any suggestion that House Cedar can directly control reach unless the story clearly frames that as the feed's assumption.
- Cut any Blink wording that sounds like Mina is explaining intent.
- Cut one optional receipt if Wave 4 feels overloaded; PLAT-08 is useful but not essential.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Define Mina's admin access in-world before it matters: scheduler, launch admin, or shared creator dashboard helper.
- Show the order plainly: settings change, weak launch window, rumor post, public clip.
- Make the first 42 minutes visually understandable as a launch window, not a data-analysis task.
- Use readable timestamp chips rather than tiny metadata.
- Clarify why Juno did not immediately know the source of the weak window.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Let Mina use common creator language rather than one repeated signature phrase.
- Make her Frame sample post plausibly supportive at first glance.
- Let Beck be abrasive but not cartoonish.
- Make the rumor account sloppy and engagement-driven rather than obviously planted.
- Make Mina's mini-drop teaser visible enough to establish benefit but not so similar that it instantly names her as the answer.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium. Mina has access, a similar product teaser, and a private scheduling message. If those appear too close together, players may select her before understanding the receipt graph. Sequence the reveal so her motive appears after the player already sees source timing and launch-window confusion.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Medium. Players may struggle with source/amplifier separation and upload-state semantics. The scenario needs simple labels, strong ordering, and report feedback that points to missing context without procedural language.

## Production Risk

Medium. The biggest production risks are fictional analytics UI, upload status readability, and bag continuity. These are manageable if the scenario avoids tiny UI details and lets stills/text carry most of the meaning.

## Wave 4 Recommendation

Move to Wave 4 as a strong but revision-dependent candidate. It has excellent cross-app semantics and a relevant hook, but it should be developed only if Wave 4 simplifies the technical layer and makes Mina less immediately obvious.
