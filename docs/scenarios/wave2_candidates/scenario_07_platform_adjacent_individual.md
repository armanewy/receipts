# Scenario 07: The Quiet Boost

## 1. Scenario Metadata

- Title: The Quiet Boost
- Category: Platform-adjacent individual manipulation
- Tone: Creator-world paranoia, career pressure, public pile-on
- Estimated playtime: 30-40 minutes
- Intended difficulty: Medium-high
- Starting app: Loop
- Apps used: Loop, Frame, Threadline, Blink
- Primary transformation types: source laundering, timestamp contradiction, public/private mismatch, amplifier/source separation, incentive trail
- One-sentence semantic promise: A creator's apparent feed suppression turns out to be a personal reach panic manufactured by someone close enough to reshape the launch window.

## 2. Surface Event Semantics

- Artifact type: Viral Loop clip with a creator speaking over a flat-lined analytics screenshot.
- Emotional charge: Public unfairness, creator vulnerability, anger at opaque platform dynamics.
- Apparent target: House Cedar, a fictional creator collective, and the unnamed "feed people" around them.
- Immediate question: Did Juno's product launch get quietly buried because she turned down a collective deal?
- Why a user keeps scrolling: Comments name a private launch group, a Threadline rumor account, and a Frame story that supposedly shows who knew the post was being muted.

## 3. Public Interpretation Semantics

- Apparent actor: House Cedar's talent lead, Beck Rivas, using influence to punish Juno Vale.
- Apparent motive: Juno declined their collective offer and made them look desperate.
- Apparent timeline: Juno declined the offer, posted her launch clip, the clip stopped reaching people, then a rumor account amplified her analytics.
- Apparent wronged party: Juno, a mid-tier creator trying to launch a handmade bag line.
- Viral supporting artifact: Cropped analytics screenshot showing a sharp reach drop after 9:05 PM.
- Emotional reason it spreads: Viewers already believe small creators get punished by invisible platform relationships.
- What it gets right: The launch clip was mishandled, the analytics screenshot is real, and Beck did pressure Juno publicly.
- What it gets wrong: Beck did not control the drop; Juno's own scheduler, Mina Ro, changed the post visibility and used platform panic to redirect attention.

## 4. Actual Causal Chain

- Responsible individual: Mina Ro, Juno's part-time scheduler and creator admin.
- Motive/pressure: Mina wanted Juno to delay the bag launch so Mina's own styling page could pitch a similar "quiet carry" mini-drop first.
- Action taken: Mina moved Juno's launch clip into a low-reach posting window, set the first version to followers-only, then pushed a cropped analytics screenshot through a rumor account.
- Manipulation/concealment method: She framed the bad launch as platform-adjacent retaliation, separated the anonymous source from the loud amplifiers, and hid the admin change behind general reach anxiety.
- True timeline: Mina saw Beck's public pressure earlier in the day, changed the Loop launch settings before the clip went public, leaked a cropped analytics screenshot from the first 42 minutes, then posted Frame hints from her own sample shoot before Juno recovered the launch.
- Intended public belief: Juno would blame House Cedar, postpone the launch, and lean harder on Mina for "recovery" strategy.
- Unintended trace left behind: Mina's Frame story shows the sample bag and the same calendar reminder before Juno's public launch, and a Blink screenshot shows she was the only person asking to "hold main until after the preview window."
- Why this explanation is more satisfying: It explains the reach dip, the rumor timing, Mina's early access to the product, and why the panic benefited one specific person instead of an abstract platform.

## 5. Transformation Types Used

- Source laundering: Mina's cropped analytics screenshot starts in a small Threadline rumor account, then becomes credible when creator commentary accounts repeat it.
- Timestamp contradiction: Loop upload status, Threadline posts, and Frame story timestamps show the leak began before Juno publicly noticed the issue.
- Public/private mismatch: Public posts say "no one knew what happened," while Blink messages show Mina had admin access and was directing launch timing.
- Amplifier/source separation: The loudest accounts are reacting to a rumor, not originating it; the source traces back to someone with dashboard access.
- Incentive trail: Mina's own styling account and sample-bag teaser show a concrete benefit from delaying Juno's launch.

## 6. App Semantic Roles

### Loop

- Role in scenario: Starts the reach-panic narrative and shows how creator reactions become viral.
- Key artifact: Juno's face-to-camera clip with the cropped analytics overlay.
- Misleading surface: A flattened chart makes the drop look like outside suppression.
- Truth it reveals: Upload version history and comments point to a visibility setting change before the public clip gained traction.
- Unlock trigger: A top comment links to a Threadline rumor account that posted the chart first.
- Adversarial concern: Analytics visuals can feel too technical if not kept simple.

### Frame

- Role in scenario: Shows social proximity, product access, and the pre-launch sample trail.
- Key artifact: Mina's story featuring the sample bag on a chair before Juno's launch.
- Misleading surface: It looks like a supportive behind-the-scenes teaser.
- Truth it reveals: Mina had the product and a calendar reminder before the alleged outside suppression.
- Unlock trigger: Juno's Loop comments mention Mina's "cute prep story."
- Adversarial concern: The bag continuity must be clear without relying on tiny pixels.

### Threadline

- Role in scenario: Maps how the platform-panic story spread and separates source from amplifier.
- Key artifact: Rumor account post with the cropped analytics image and a vague "from someone close."
- Misleading surface: The discourse makes House Cedar look like the origin of the problem.
- Truth it reveals: The rumor post predates Juno's public complaint and uses wording Mina repeats elsewhere.
- Unlock trigger: The rumor thread references a private launch chat screenshot.
- Adversarial concern: Too much text could slow the mobile loop.

### Blink

- Role in scenario: Reveals private launch coordination without handing the answer directly to the player.
- Key artifact: Launch group screenshot where Mina suggests holding "main" for a preview window.
- Misleading surface: It reads like routine social scheduling advice.
- Truth it reveals: Mina had timing control, knew the launch window, and shaped the first 42 minutes.
- Unlock trigger: Threadline replies mention a cropped Blink screenshot in Juno's close circle.
- Adversarial concern: Private content must not feel omniscient or too convenient.

## 7. Progressive App Disclosure

- Starting app and why: Loop starts because the viral reach-panic clip has immediate emotional pull.
- Unlock 2 trigger: Loop comments point to the Threadline rumor account that posted the analytics first.
- Unlock 3 trigger: Threadline replies reference Mina's Frame story with the sample bag.
- Unlock 4 trigger: Frame story reactions mention the private launch chat on Blink.
- What question each unlock answers: Threadline answers "where did the chart come from"; Frame answers "who had early product access"; Blink answers "who shaped the launch timing."
- What new question each unlock creates: Threadline asks why the source was early; Frame asks why Mina was previewing the sample; Blink asks whether Mina's scheduling advice connects to the reach drop.

## 9. Contradiction Ladder

- Surface belief: Juno's launch was buried after House Cedar pressure.
- First wobble: The analytics screenshot appeared publicly before Juno framed the issue on Loop.
- Cross-app context: Mina's Frame story shows pre-launch access and an early "preview window."
- Timeline break: Loop upload history and Blink timing show the weak window came from a visibility setting, not a later reaction.
- Source/method link: Threadline wording and Blink timing connect the anonymous source to someone with admin access.
- Motive/benefit: Mina's own styling page benefits from Juno delaying the bag launch.
- Confirmation bundle: PLAT-03, PLAT-04, PLAT-07, PLAT-09, and PLAT-10 support WHO, WHY, WHEN, and HOW together.

## 10. Report Requirements

- Correct WHO: Mina Ro.
- Correct WHY: She wanted to delay Juno's bag launch and create space for her own similar styling drop.
- Correct WHEN: Before and during the first 42 minutes of Juno's Loop launch window.
- Correct HOW: She changed launch visibility/timing, leaked a cropped analytics screenshot, and let amplifiers blame House Cedar.
- Minimum required receipts: PLAT-03, PLAT-04, PLAT-07, PLAT-09, PLAT-10.
- Optional supporting receipts: PLAT-05, PLAT-06, PLAT-08, PLAT-11.
- Accepted substitute receipt bundles: PLAT-06 plus PLAT-11 can substitute for PLAT-09 only if PLAT-07 and PLAT-10 are also saved.

## 11. Plausible Wrong Theories

### Beck Rivas punished Juno through House Cedar

- Why plausible: Beck publicly pressured Juno and creator audiences already distrust collective politics.
- What it explains: The public anger and why House Cedar becomes the feed's target.
- What it fails to explain: The early rumor timestamp, the upload visibility split, and Mina's sample-bag benefit.
- Social-native feedback: "This explains why people picked a side fast, but it does not explain who had the chart before Juno posted."

### Juno staged the reach panic herself

- Why plausible: The viral clip helped Juno recover attention.
- What it explains: Juno's emotional post and the reposted launch momentum.
- What it fails to explain: Mina's private launch advice and pre-launch sample activity.
- Social-native feedback: "The outrage helped Juno later, but the first bad window still points back to someone else handling the launch."

### The rumor account invented the whole chart

- Why plausible: Anonymous Threadline accounts often exaggerate for engagement.
- What it explains: Source laundering and overconfident quote-posts.
- What it fails to explain: The matching Loop upload history and Juno's real dashboard crop.
- Social-native feedback: "The rumor account warped the story, but the chart was not made from nothing."

## 12. Ending Semantics

- Public story: Juno was punished by creator-world gatekeepers and saved by audience pressure.
- True story: Mina used real platform anxiety and her admin access to create a weak launch window, then let the feed blame a bigger target.
- Emotional payoff: The player gets the satisfaction of seeing a messy, personal incentive trail under a huge public explanation.
- Why the false story spread: It gave people a familiar villain, a simple platform-adjacent grievance, and a shareable chart.
- How the feed reacts after the report: Some accounts quietly pivot to "check your team before blaming the feed," while Juno posts a restrained update about changing her launch access.

## 14. Production Feasibility Notes

- Realistic photos needed: 4-6, mostly product/sample bag, Mina's profile grid, Juno's workspace.
- Short videos needed: 2, Juno's viral talking-head clip and a short launch/repost clip.
- Comments/text content needed: 30-45 comments/posts across Loop and Threadline, plus 1 compact Blink chat.
- Risky assets: Analytics screen must feel readable but fictional; sample bag continuity must be clear without brand marks.
- Assets that can be made from stills: Frame stories, profile grids, Threadline screenshots, analytics overlay.
- Assets that require careful continuity: Sample bag, timestamps, Loop upload status, Mina's profile teaser timing.

## 15. Self-Adversarial Critique

- What is too obvious: Mina's sample-bag story could point to her too quickly if it is shown before the Threadline source timing.
- What is too hidden: The upload visibility split may be too platform-specific unless the UI copy is plain.
- What app feels unnecessary: Blink risks feeling like a late proof dump unless its chat is teased earlier by Threadline.
- What receipt feels too confession-like: PLAT-10 must stay as scheduling advice, not Mina admitting intent.
- Where players might get stuck: Differentiating source, amplifier, and beneficiary across Threadline could be text-heavy.
- What feels unrealistic: A scheduler leaking analytics is plausible, but the scenario needs clear access boundaries.
- What should be changed before playtesting: Simplify the analytics UI, delay Mina's profile teaser until after players see her supportive public role, and make Beck's pressure true but incomplete.
