## 1. Mystery Structure Critic

The public interpretation is strong because it gives the player an easy first judgment: Mara appears to move on too quickly while Jace looks wounded. The actual causal chain explains more than the surface story: why Mara stayed vague, why the jacket reading fails, why the fan thread timing matters, and why Jace benefits from a clean sympathy wave.

The contradiction ladder is mostly sound. It starts with a visual wobble, moves into cross-app timing, then adds source/method and motive. The biggest structural risk is that the thread timestamp before Jace's clip may make Jace feel responsible too quickly. The scenario should make the early timestamp feel suspicious but incomplete until the private delay request and showcase benefit are paired with it.

The solution does not depend on one receipt, but S01-R07 is close to overpowering the rest if written too directly. The responsible person appears early enough through Jace's public clip and private timing request. Wrong theories are plausible, especially the Sol jacket read and the fan-account-only read. The report can resist brute force if each slot requires a different kind of receipt: timing, jacket context, source path, and benefit.

Perspective score: 4.2 / 5

## 2. Doomscroll/UX Critic

The first 30 seconds are strong. A cropped clip, a sad breakup post, and visible comment disagreement create immediate pull without needing setup. The app unlocks feel motivated because comments naturally point to Frame and Threadline, and later screenshots point to Blink.

The scenario feels like browsing rather than a puzzle as long as timestamps are visible through ordinary social surfaces and not treated like hidden metadata. The "one more thing" motivation is excellent: the player wants to check the jacket source, then the Friday story, then the fan thread timing, then the private context.

The report screen could interrupt the fantasy if it asks players to make a formal argument too early. It should feel like assembling a social theory from saved posts, not filling a worksheet. Each app creates a new desire to continue, with Blink carrying the highest risk of feeling too convenient if it opens too much private context at once.

Perspective score: 4.5 / 5

## 3. Social Realism Critic

The behavior is broadly believable. Creator breakup timing, queued couple content, fan accounts over-reading jackets, and sympathy-driven audience shifts all fit the product's social drama target. Mara's vague Frame story feels plausible if she is bound by a private timing agreement and does not want to feed the pile-on.

The least realistic element is the exact sequence of Jace benefiting from a showcase slot immediately after the sympathy wave. That can work, but it needs to feel like a visible incentive rather than a cartoon reward. The fan account also needs prior behavior so it does not read as a plot device.

Receipts fit the app surfaces well. Loop carries the misleading visual, Frame carries relationship timing, Threadline carries public spread, and Blink carries private mismatch. The true theory is emotionally believable because it rests on reputation management rather than extreme behavior.

Perspective score: 4.0 / 5

## 4. Production Feasibility Critic

The production burden is manageable. It needs three controlled vertical clips, several story/still assets, text threads, and short private exchanges. Most of the heavy lifting can be handled with stills, comments, and readable timestamps.

Continuity risk centers on the orange jacket, Studio 6 context, Jace's room/outfit timing, and timestamps across Loop and Threadline. The jacket must be readable as an object without relying on tiny visual detail. Jace's emotional clip must avoid melodrama; restrained performance will help.

The most expensive asset is the Loop clip sequence because it needs both a misleading crop and a fuller context version. The rest can be screenshots or still-based content. App Store/IP sensitivity is low if all app names, UI, and branding stay fictional.

Perspective score: 4.0 / 5

## 5. Ethical/Safety Critic

The scenario avoids real people, public figures, and real platform branding. It is contained as fictional creator drama. The main safety concern is pile-on energy: the player should not feel encouraged to harass Mara or Jace, and the ending should reduce blame heat rather than reward public shaming.

The lurker framing remains contained because the player is browsing authored surfaces and saving receipts already present in the scenario. Blink content should feel unlocked through in-world leaks or screenshots, not like the player is invading private accounts.

Perspective score: 4.0 / 5

## 6. Product Fit Critic

This matches the north star well. It is a mobile-first social browsing mystery with receipts, cross-app comparison, and a structured theory/report. It avoids restricted product-facing vocabulary in the scenario draft and keeps the player in a lurker role.

All four app archetypes earn their place. Loop is the hook, Frame is the relationship-timing surface, Threadline is the public spread surface, and Blink is the private/public mismatch surface. The scenario is not interchangeable because the orange jacket and Sunday timing give it a memorable social symbol.

Perspective score: 4.4 / 5

## Top 5 Strengths

1. Strong first-scroll hook with breakup ambiguity and a visual symbol.
2. Excellent cross-app flow where each unlock answers one question and creates another.
3. Clean report mapping across WHO, WHY, WHEN, HOW, and receipt bundle.
4. Receipts have apparent meanings that change with context instead of flipping from false to true.
5. Production is feasible with a small cast, controlled clips, and text/still-heavy surfaces.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. Jace may become too obvious once the fan thread timestamp predates his clip.
2. S01-R07 could overpower the graph if the private message is written too cleanly.
3. The showcase benefit risks feeling too convenient if it appears as a direct payoff.
4. The fan account source link may feel thin if it relies mostly on repeated phrasing.
5. Eden's bracelet side path may distract from the core Mara/Jace/Sol theory.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Make @softwatcher feel like a recurring account with prior fan behavior before the soft-launch thread.
- Keep Jace's Blink message limited to delay/timing; do not let it imply he planned the pile-on.
- Make the showcase benefit visible but indirect, such as increased consideration or reposted support before the roster lands.
- Add a non-private public artifact that hints Jace had access to the cropped clip before the fan thread.
- Give Mara's Friday story a concrete reason for ambiguity without making her sound like she is explaining the plot.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut or downgrade Eden's bracelet receipt if it pulls attention away from the core theory.
- Cut any comment that directly says Jace planted the thread.
- Cut any Blink wording that explains Jace's motive in one line.
- Cut any production dependency on tiny jacket markings, background text, or unreadable timestamps.
- Cut duplicate discourse posts if Threadline starts to feel like recap rather than feed behavior.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Make the Loop and Threadline timestamps large enough to read as normal app context.
- Clarify that Sol's jacket context is discoverable through captions/comments, not hidden visual inspection.
- Keep the Thursday private split, Friday vague story, Sunday fan thread, and Monday showcase order easy to compare.
- Show why Mara could not simply correct the public story immediately.
- Make the accepted receipt bundle clear enough that players understand why each slot needs support.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Let comments disagree naturally instead of steering players toward the correct read.
- Make @softwatcher's language echo Jace's style lightly, not identically.
- Present Jace's benefit as social momentum and opportunity timing, not a villain monologue.
- Keep Mara's posts emotionally restrained rather than overly noble.
- Let the jacket reframe land through social context, not an explicit "it was Sol's jacket" reveal alone.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium. The early Threadline timestamp plus Jace's delay request can point too directly to Jace. The fix is to make each receipt narrow: the timestamp suggests odd sourcing, the delay request explains public timing, the jacket snap removes one false read, and the showcase post explains benefit.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Low to medium. Players may miss timestamp ordering if it is visually buried or if app time formats differ. The comparison should be surfaced through ordinary comments and saved-receipt summaries without sounding like a hint system.

## Production Risk

Moderate. The scenario needs believable creator footage and strong continuity around a jacket, room setup, and posting order. It is still practical because most receipts can be text or still-image assets, and the critical visual detail can be made large and repeated.
