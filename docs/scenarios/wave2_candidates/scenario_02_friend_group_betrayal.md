# Scenario 02: The Empty Fifth Chair

## 1. Scenario Metadata

- Title: The Empty Fifth Chair
- Category: Friend-group betrayal
- Tone: Public friend breakup, status anxiety, clipped private context
- Estimated playtime: 30-40 minutes
- Intended difficulty: Medium
- Starting app: Frame
- Apps used: Loop, Frame, Threadline, Blink
- Primary transformation types: Selective cropping, source laundering, temporal inversion, public/private mismatch, role reversal, incentive trail
- One-sentence semantic promise: A group publicly cuts Rhea for supposedly leaking private chat screenshots, but the receipts show Mina shaped the fallout to protect a solo opportunity built from the group's shared idea.

## 2. Surface Event Semantics

- Artifact type: Frame story carousel showing four friends at a dinner table with an empty fifth chair and the caption "some people screenshot everything."
- Emotional charge: Exclusion, betrayal, group loyalty, and public awkwardness.
- Apparent target: Rhea Vale, the missing fifth friend.
- Immediate question: Did Rhea leak the group's Blink chat and get cut from the circle?
- Why a user keeps scrolling: The story points to a Loop table clip, Threadline has the leaked screenshot, and comments argue over whether Rhea deserved the empty chair.

## 3. Public Interpretation Semantics

- Apparent actor: Rhea.
- Apparent motive: She was jealous after being left out of a creator dinner and wanted to embarrass the group.
- Apparent timeline: Rhea leaks private Blink chat, the group removes her from the Frame highlight, then she storms out of Mina's birthday dinner.
- Apparent wronged party: Mina Saye and the remaining friends in the supper-club circle.
- Viral supporting artifact: A Threadline screenshot of the private chat paired with a Loop clip of Rhea leaving the table.
- Emotional reason it spreads: The feed gets a clean loyalty story: private-chat leaker versus hurt friend group.
- What it gets right: A private chat screenshot did become public, and Rhea had conflict with Mina.
- What it gets wrong: Mina leaked and cropped the chat first, removed Rhea from the public friend image before the leak spread, and used the fallout to secure a solo hosting slot.

## 4. Actual Causal Chain

- Responsible individual: Mina Saye.
- Motive/pressure: Mina wanted to keep a solo supper-club hosting opportunity while hiding that she reused the group's shared pitch without credit.
- Action taken: She cropped a Blink group chat from her own view, sent it to a small Threadline account, removed Rhea from the Frame highlight before the leak went public, and let Rhea look like the person who broke trust.
- Manipulation/concealment method: Selective cropping hid Mina's harsh comments and Rhea's de-escalation, while source laundering made the leak seem audience-discovered.
- True timeline:
  - Tuesday afternoon: Rhea privately asks Mina why the Cedar Room pitch uses the group's shared supper-club concept.
  - Tuesday night: Mina saves and crops the Blink chat, leaving out her own comments.
  - Wednesday morning: Mina removes Rhea from the Frame highlight and drafts the empty-chair story.
  - Wednesday afternoon: @porchwatch posts the cropped screenshot on Threadline.
  - Wednesday evening: A Loop clip of Rhea leaving dinner is reposted as if it happened after the leak, though the dinner was the night before.
  - Thursday: Mina is announced as a solo host for the Cedar Room supper series.
- Intended public belief: Rhea leaked private chat because she was jealous and then acted dramatic when confronted.
- Unintended trace left behind: Frame archive timing shows Mina cut Rhea before the public leak, the Blink crop matches Mina's chat view, and the Cedar Room announcement explains Mina's benefit.
- Why this explanation is more satisfying: It explains why the group post was ready too early, why the leak crop protects Mina, why Rhea's private messages sound calmer than the public story, and why Mina gained status afterward.

## 5. Transformation Types Used

- Selective cropping: The leaked Blink screenshot hides Mina's harsh comments and Rhea asking to slow the conversation down.
- Source laundering: @porchwatch makes Mina's leak look like outside gossip instead of an inside move.
- Temporal inversion: The Loop dinner clip is framed as a reaction to the leak, but it happened before the Threadline post spread.
- Public/private mismatch: Mina's public "protect your circle" stance conflicts with her private pitch behavior.
- Role reversal: Rhea appears to betray the group, but she was the person asking for credit and context.
- Incentive trail: Mina's solo Cedar Room announcement gives a concrete reason to shift blame.

## 6. App Semantic Roles

### Loop

- Role in scenario: Provides the emotional moving image of Rhea leaving dinner.
- Key artifact: Reposted clip of Rhea standing up during Mina's toast.
- Misleading surface: Rhea appears to be storming out after being confronted about the leak.
- Truth it reveals: Comments and repost timing show the clip is from Tuesday dinner, before the leak thread went public.
- Unlock trigger: Frame story links to the Loop clip, and Loop comments mention the Threadline screenshot.
- Adversarial concern: The clip should not require players to inspect tiny background calendars; timestamp and caption context should be legible.

### Frame

- Role in scenario: Establishes group status, exclusion, and Mina's public curation.
- Key artifact: Empty-fifth-chair story and removed shared highlight.
- Misleading surface: Mina looks like a hurt friend setting a boundary.
- Truth it reveals: Mina removed Rhea before the public leak, which breaks the stated timeline.
- Unlock trigger: Frame comments and tags point to the Loop table clip and the Cedar Room story.
- Adversarial concern: The empty-chair post could feel too symbolic if not paired with casual group content.

### Threadline

- Role in scenario: Shows the leak, source chain, and discourse pile-on.
- Key artifact: @porchwatch cropped Blink screenshot.
- Misleading surface: The screenshot seems to come from Rhea or someone defending her.
- Truth it reveals: The crop protects Mina and uses phrasing that links back to Mina's posts.
- Unlock trigger: The thread includes a screenshot with a Blink group name that unlocks the private context.
- Adversarial concern: The text layer must feel like public chatter, not an explanation dump.

### Blink

- Role in scenario: Reveals private context, the uncropped chat, and Rhea's actual tone.
- Key artifact: Uncropped supper-club group chat and Rhea's one-on-one message to Mina.
- Misleading surface: Early cropped snippets suggest Rhea was saving messages to use later.
- Truth it reveals: Mina had the screenshot view, made the sharpest comments, and had been challenged about the pitch.
- Unlock trigger: Threadline screenshot exposes the Blink group name and contact labels.
- Adversarial concern: Blink should not hand over Mina's entire motive in one message; it should provide context that needs the Frame and Threadline receipts.

## 7. Progressive App Disclosure

- Starting app and why: Frame starts with public social exclusion, the strongest friend-group visual.
- Unlock 2 trigger: The empty-chair story reposts the Loop table clip.
- Unlock 3 trigger: Loop comments point to @porchwatch's Threadline post as the reason everyone chose sides.
- Unlock 4 trigger: The Threadline screenshot shows the Blink group name and partial contact labels, opening the private message context.
- What question each unlock answers:
  - Loop answers what people think Rhea did in person.
  - Threadline answers what screenshot made the group turn public.
  - Blink answers what the private chat looked like before it was cropped.
- What new question each unlock creates:
  - Loop creates the question of when the dinner clip actually happened.
  - Threadline creates the question of who had the screenshot view.
  - Blink creates the question of why Mina needed Rhea out before the Cedar Room announcement.

## 9. Contradiction Ladder

- Surface belief: Rhea leaked the private chat and got removed from the group.
- First wobble: The Frame highlight change appears before @porchwatch's public leak.
- Cross-app context: Loop comments say the dramatic dinner clip happened before the leak thread.
- Timeline break: Rhea's Frame place card and the Tuesday clip conflict with the public Wednesday fallout story.
- Source/method link: The Threadline leak crop protects Mina and matches Mina's language and Blink view.
- Motive/benefit: Mina's Cedar Room solo announcement uses the group's concept and arrives right after Rhea is discredited.
- Confirmation bundle: The uncropped Blink chat, Rhea's DM about credit, the highlight timing, and the source-chain posts support the full report.

## 11. Plausible Wrong Theories

### Wrong Theory A: Rhea leaked the chat because she was jealous.

- Why plausible: The cropped screenshot centers her name, and the empty-chair post frames her as disloyal.
- What it explains: The public exclusion, the group anger, and the Loop clip.
- What it fails to explain: The early highlight change, the uncropped chat, and Mina's Cedar Room benefit.
- Social-native feedback: "This fits the empty chair, but it does not explain why the chair was empty before the thread hit."

### Wrong Theory B: @porchwatch is an outside gossip account that caused the whole spiral.

- Why plausible: The account is the loudest public source and posts the viral screenshot.
- What it explains: How the screenshot reached Threadline and why comments piled on.
- What it fails to explain: The screenshot view, Mina's phrasing, and the private pitch conflict.
- Social-native feedback: "The account made it travel, but it does not explain who had that crop."

### Wrong Theory C: The whole group quietly agreed to cut Rhea before anything leaked.

- Why plausible: The Frame story shows a united front, and no one publicly defends Rhea at first.
- What it explains: The coordinated empty-chair image and highlight rename.
- What it fails to explain: Rhea's de-escalating Blink replies and Mina's solo incentive.
- Social-native feedback: "The group followed the post, but the receipts point to one person setting the table."

### Wrong Theory D: Dax filmed the table clip to embarrass Rhea.

- Why plausible: Dax posted the Loop clip and kept it online during the pile-on.
- What it explains: Why the clip became emotional fuel.
- What it fails to explain: The cropped Blink screenshot, Mina's highlight timing, and the Cedar Room opportunity.
- Social-native feedback: "Dax amplified the scene, but the screenshot and timing started somewhere else."

## 12. Ending Semantics

- Public story: Rhea leaked private chat, got cut, and made a scene at dinner.
- True story: Mina cropped and laundered the private chat to protect a solo opportunity, then used Frame and Loop timing to make Rhea look like the disloyal friend.
- Emotional payoff: The player sees a public "protect your circle" story flip into a status move hidden inside friend-group language.
- Why the false story spread: The empty chair was visually simple, the screenshot felt private, and the Loop clip made Rhea look emotional.
- How the feed reacts after the report: Some friends quietly remove the empty-chair story, @porchwatch locks replies, and comments shift from "Rhea leaked" to "the timeline was off."

## 14. Production Feasibility Notes

- Realistic photos needed: 5-7, including empty-chair dinner image, group highlight thumbnails, Tuesday dinner still, Cedar Room announcement, and casual group history.
- Short videos needed: 1-2 controlled Loop clips, mainly Rhea leaving the table and optional repost reactions.
- Comments/text content needed: 20-30 Loop comments, 10-15 Threadline posts/replies, 2 Frame captions, 2-3 Blink exchanges.
- Risky assets: The dinner clip could feel staged if too polished; the Blink crop must be readable without feeling like a UI puzzle.
- Assets that can be made from stills: Frame stories, Threadline posts, Blink chats, Cedar Room announcement, highlight thumbnails.
- Assets that require careful continuity: Dinner date order, group names, Mina's recurring phrases, chat view/crop consistency, the supper-club concept language.

## 15. Self-Adversarial Critique

- What is too obvious: Mina's Cedar Room benefit may point too directly to her if revealed too early.
- What is too hidden: The screenshot-view detail risks feeling too subtle unless paired with the uncropped chat and Mina's language pattern.
- What app feels unnecessary: Loop risks becoming just an out-of-order clip unless comments and timestamps make it a real timeline turn.
- What receipt feels too confession-like: S02-R08 could be too clean if Rhea names the entire pitch issue; it should be specific but incomplete.
- Where players might get stuck: Players may not understand why the highlight rename timing matters unless the public leak timestamp is prominent.
- What feels unrealistic: The group staying silent while Mina controls the story needs social pressure, such as not wanting to lose the Cedar Room invite.
- What should change before playtesting: Make the group friendship texture warmer before the fracture so the empty chair has emotional weight, and make @porchwatch's source link suggestive rather than overly tidy.
