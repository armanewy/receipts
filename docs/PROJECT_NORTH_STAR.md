# Project North Star

## Product Vision

Receipts is a solo mobile-first lurker mystery experience where players browse fictional social apps, save revealing social artifacts, and assemble a theory about what really happened. It should feel like falling down a feed rabbit hole with authored payoff: the player follows social signals, compares context across apps, and builds a structured report using WHO, WHY, WHEN, HOW, and RECEIPTS.

## What It Should Feel Like

- Doomscrolling with purpose.
- Browsing familiar but fictional social spaces.
- Noticing inconsistencies that make the player tap one more profile, thread, story, or DM.
- Saving receipts because a post, clip, story, comment, or profile detail might matter later.
- Watching a public story slowly become less stable as cross-app context accumulates.
- Arranging a theory that feels like social understanding rather than formal procedure.

## What It Should Not Feel Like

- A traditional puzzle game, board game, pixel game, or hidden-object scene.
- A professional inquiry, procedural case board, or law-enforcement fantasy.
- A single obvious clue hunt.
- A set of formulaic story templates.
- A feed simulator with no authored payoff.
- A real-platform parody that copies names, marks, or exact UI.

## Locked Decisions

- The player is a lurker.
- The core loop is browsing, saving receipts, comparing context, and forming a report.
- Product-facing vocabulary must avoid detective and law-enforcement framing.
- Apps are fictional analogs of familiar social surfaces.
- Working placeholder app names are Loop, Frame, Threadline, and Blink.
- Receipts are social artifacts, not forensic evidence.
- Scenario generation must use semantic proof graphs rather than canned sentence templates.
- Scenarios should support broad internet drama, not only disappearances or crime-first stories.
- Most scenarios should resolve around an individual responsible party.
- Major scenario and design artifacts must include adversarial critique.

## Current Uncertain Decisions

- Final app names and visual identity.
- Whether the report screen should feel like a structured theory board, a social recap, an expose draft, or another in-world surface.
- How many apps should be available at the start versus unlocked through content.
- Ideal scenario length for MVP.
- How much media realism is needed before the loop feels compelling.
- How strict report validation should be.
- Whether endings should be binary, graded, or socially reactive.

## Scenario-Generation Principles

- Start from meaning, not prose.
- Define the public story the feed encourages before defining the real causal chain.
- Make the actual causal chain explain more than the public interpretation.
- Build receipts as nodes in a graph with apparent meaning, actual meaning, dependencies, and report relevance.
- Use red herrings that are true-but-incomplete rather than arbitrary fakeouts.
- Require cross-app comparison for meaningful progress.
- Let app unlocks emerge from content references, handles, screenshots, profile links, comments, and social context.
- Avoid direct confession as the only proof.
- Design wrong theories that feel socially plausible but fail to explain key contradictions.

## Adversarial Critic

The biggest risks are that the product becomes a conventional mystery with social media skin, that scenario outputs become formulaic, or that the content feels like fake drama without a strong semantic backbone. Other major risks include overreliance on tiny visual details, app surfaces that do not earn their place, direct-confession solutions, unrealistic posting behavior, and endings that encourage real-world callout energy instead of contained fictional play.
