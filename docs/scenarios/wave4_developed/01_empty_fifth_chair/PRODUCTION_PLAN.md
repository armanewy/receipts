## Estimated Content Counts

- Realistic photos needed: 8-10.
- Short videos needed: 1 essential Loop clip, 1 optional reaction/repost clip.
- Story screenshots needed: 5-6.
- Text posts needed: 12-16 across Threadline and profiles.
- Comments needed: 35-50 across Loop, Frame, and Threadline.
- DMs/snaps needed: 4-6 Blink artifacts.
- Profiles needed: 6-8 lightweight account/profile surfaces.
- Receipt-critical artifacts: 9 required, 6 optional/supporting.

## Photo And Story Assets

- Empty-chair birthday dinner story.
- Old "Supper Five" warm group carousel with Rhea contributing to menu cards.
- Renamed "Supper Four" highlight screenshot.
- Tuesday dinner still with Rhea's place card or gift bag.
- Cedar Room solo host announcement.
- Mina profile update with Cedar Room reference.
- Optional group-history thumbnails for warmth.

## Short Video Assets

- Essential: Dax's vertical table clip showing Rhea leaving during Mina's toast.
- Optional: A repost/reaction clip that shows the clip's spread without adding new facts.

The essential clip should be short and controlled. It should not require strong acting. Use framing, captions, and comment timing to carry meaning.

## Text And Comment Assets

- Loop comments should include mixed reactions: quick judgment, date correction, jokes, sympathy, and links to @porchwatch.
- Threadline posts should show source and amplifier separation without becoming a long explainer thread.
- Frame comments should feel like birthday/support comments with vague loyalty language.
- Blink messages should be short, slightly messy, and private-feeling. No one should say the full scenario truth directly.

## Continuity Objects Needed

- Empty chair.
- Fifth place card or gift bag for Rhea.
- Handwritten menu cards from the shared supper concept.
- Cedar Room poster language that echoes the group concept.
- Group chat name: "Supper Five Planning."
- Mina's recurring table/seat language, varied enough to avoid an exact copy-paste tell.
- Consistent date order: Tuesday dinner, Wednesday public crop spread, Thursday Cedar Room reveal.

## Visual Motifs

- Tables, place cards, menus, candles, and shared dishes.
- Warm group-history colors contrasted with colder empty-chair framing.
- Repeated table language in captions and posts.
- Partial crops and screenshots that feel socially messy rather than designed.

## Asset Risks

- The Loop table clip could feel staged if the performance is too dramatic.
- The screenshot-source receipt could feel like a tiny UI puzzle if not supported by language and timing receipts.
- The Cedar Room opportunity could make Mina too obvious if introduced before the player has absorbed the public theory.
- The group silence could feel unrealistic unless Nia/Sol context and event-access pressure are included.
- Too many table metaphors could make the writing feel authored rather than social-native.

## Easiest Assets

- Threadline text posts and replies.
- Frame profile/highlight screenshots.
- Blink chat excerpts.
- Cedar Room announcement poster.
- Old group-history stills using recurring props.

## Hardest Assets

- The table exit clip.
- Any image requiring five-person continuity across old and current group assets.
- Crop/uncrop continuity for Blink screenshots.
- Keeping Mina's phrasing recognizable but not too tidy.

## Receipts That Should Not Rely On Tiny Visual Detail

- S02-R11 should be readable through multiple supports: crop edge, contact order, admin placement, source-chain language, and the fact that Mina's removed lines are exactly what the crop hides.
- S02-R02 should show timing through obvious archive/profile timestamps, not subtle UI decoration.
- S02-R04 should show timing through repost date, caption context, and comments, not background props.
- S02-R12 should show benefit through clear announcement language, not small poster text.

## Realistic-But-Fictional People Safety

- All people, handles, venues, and app surfaces must be fictional.
- Do not use real public figures, influencers, venue names, or app branding.
- Avoid lookalike prompts or references for cast generation.
- Use consistent fictional cast reference sheets when producing media later.
- Prefer partial faces, hands, table objects, menus, and story-style framing where continuity is hard.
- Keep the ending contained inside the fictional feed reaction; do not make it feel like an invitation for real-world pile-on behavior.
