# MVP Adversarial Review

## What Still Might Fail

The scenario can fail if the empty-chair hook lands as a generic exclusion image instead of a personal group rupture. It needs enough warm "Supper Five" context in the first few minutes for the public cut to matter emotionally.

It can also fail if Mina becomes too obvious before the player has believed the Rhea story. Mina must first read as a hurt host, not as the obvious answer.

## Where Users Might Get Bored

- Too much Threadline explanation before the player reaches Blink.
- Too many old group-history artifacts before the first contradiction.
- Too many Cedar Room references before the player understands why Thursday matters.
- Long Blink chats that explain rather than imply.

## Where Users Might Get Confused

- The difference between dinner date, repost date, and screenshot spread date.
- Why @porchwatch is an amplifier but not the full answer.
- Why the crop-source alignment points toward Mina without acting alone.
- Why the other friends stay quiet if Rhea was treated unfairly.
- Why Cedar Room creates pressure without becoming the WHO answer.

## Report Brute-Force Risk

Moderate. Mina is central from the opening artifact, so some players may try her early.

Mitigations:

- Require supported WHY and HOW, not only WHO.
- Make early Mina plausible as hurt host.
- Keep @porchwatch and Dax credible as partial answers.
- Make Cedar Room visible before meaningful, so it reads as background until paired with Rhea's DM.
- Feedback should tell players which social context is missing.

## App Unlock Organic Fit

The unlock path is strong:

- Frame to Loop through a repost sticker and comments.
- Loop to Threadline through @porchwatch references.
- Threadline to Blink through the group-chat name and crop questions.
- Blink back to Frame through Cedar Room references.

The main risk is overusing comments as unlock keys. Content writing should diversify with repost stickers, visible handles, tags, group names, profile links, and story screenshots.

## Social Behavior Realism

Mostly strong. Friend-group curation, private chat crops, small gossip accounts, public silence, and opportunity pressure are all believable.

Needs care:

- Mina should not act like a cartoon strategist.
- Rhea should not be perfectly noble.
- Dax should not sound like he exists only to move the Loop clip.
- Nia's silence should feel uncomfortable but human.

## Does It Feel Like A Game?

Risk is moderate around S02-R11. If crop-source alignment is presented as a mechanical matching task, the experience could feel puzzle-like.

The fix is to make S02-R11 a receipt bundle: crop edge plus source-chain language plus missing chat lines plus timing, not a tiny UI comparison.

## Does It Feel Too Passive?

Risk is low. The player moves between apps, saves receipts, revisits prior surfaces, and compares timing/context. The report requires active assembly.

The first slice must still avoid passivity by giving the player a choice to tap Mina's highlight, the Loop repost, comments, and @porchwatch references.

## Is The Content Burden Too High?

No for MVP. The scenario needs one essential clip, a manageable set of stills, and mostly text/story/chat assets.

The highest burden is social realism, not asset quantity. The posts and comments must feel casual and varied, especially in the first 5-8 minutes.

## Top 10 Revisions Before Content Writing

1. Define the exact age/context of the friend group and Cedar Room scene.
2. Write a tighter Mina/Rhea relationship note before generating posts.
3. Decide how warm the "Supper Five" history should feel in the first two minutes.
4. Make Rhea imperfect but not disloyal in public-facing content.
5. Keep Cedar Room pressure indirect until after the first timing contradiction.
6. Design @porchwatch as a believable small account with normal posts, not only the screenshot thread.
7. Split S02-R11 across multiple readable supports.
8. Make Dax's clip emotionally convincing but not an answer surface.
9. Establish why Nia and Sol stay quiet without making the whole group responsible.
10. Draft report feedback in social-native language before writing final content.

## Ready For Human Review

Ready for human review: yes.

## Biggest Reason To Proceed

It has the strongest all-around MVP balance: readable hook, graph-like receipts, all four apps earning their place, and feasible production.

## Biggest Reason To Hesitate

The scenario depends on social texture. If the group world feels fake or underwritten, the empty chair loses emotional force and the receipt graph becomes too mechanical.
