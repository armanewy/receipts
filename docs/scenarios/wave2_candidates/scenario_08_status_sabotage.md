# Scenario 08: Empty Chair Energy

## 1. Scenario Metadata

- Title: Empty Chair Energy
- Category: Status sabotage
- Tone: Friend-group/workshop tension, public embarrassment, social standing loss
- Estimated playtime: 25-35 minutes
- Intended difficulty: Medium
- Starting app: Frame
- Apps used: Frame, Loop, Threadline, Blink
- Primary transformation types: selective cropping, temporal inversion, public/private mismatch, role reversal, context collapse
- One-sentence semantic promise: A missing speaker looks like a flaky social climber until cross-app receipts show a friend quietly redirected the invite path to take the spotlight.

## 2. Surface Event Semantics

- Artifact type: Frame story carousel from a small creator workshop showing an empty speaker chair with a pointed caption.
- Emotional charge: Public embarrassment, betrayal, secondhand awkwardness, status loss.
- Apparent target: Priya Sen, a collage artist who supposedly skipped a community showcase after asking for a featured slot.
- Immediate question: Did Priya ghost the showcase after making everyone plan around her?
- Why a user keeps scrolling: The comments mention a Loop clip of the awkward moment, a Threadline recap, and a Blink group chat that "makes it worse."

## 3. Public Interpretation Semantics

- Apparent actor: Priya Sen.
- Apparent motive: She wanted the prestige of being listed but bailed when a bigger invite came along.
- Apparent timeline: Priya asked for the feature, accepted the slot, stopped replying, then failed to show up while the group scrambled.
- Apparent wronged party: Noor Vale, the organizer, and the workshop members who had to cover the empty slot.
- Viral supporting artifact: Cropped Frame story showing Priya's name card on an empty chair beside Noor's caption: "some people love the flyer more than the room."
- Emotional reason it spreads: People enjoy calling out status-chasing behavior in small creative communities.
- What it gets right: Priya did miss the showcase, and the group did have a public awkward moment.
- What it gets wrong: Priya never received the final venue change; Tess Malik redirected the update thread and used the empty chair to claim Priya's panel slot.

## 4. Actual Causal Chain

- Responsible individual: Tess Malik, Priya's friend and fellow workshop member.
- Motive/pressure: Tess wanted the featured speaker slot and resented Priya getting listed on the flyer after a late portfolio review.
- Action taken: Tess forwarded Priya the old venue link, muted Priya from the final Blink update thread, cropped the empty-chair story, and seeded the Threadline recap that framed Priya as a no-show.
- Manipulation/concealment method: She collapsed context by showing the empty chair without the venue-change thread, reversed the role by presenting herself as the responsible replacement, and let public comments turn logistics into character judgment.
- True timeline: Priya accepted the slot, Tess noticed the venue changed, Tess kept Priya on the old address thread, Priya arrived at the wrong room, Tess stepped in at the real room, then the cropped empty-chair story spread.
- Intended public belief: Priya chased status and wasted everyone's time.
- Unintended trace left behind: Priya's old-location check-in, Tess's forwarded outdated address, and the Blink update list show Priya was left off the final route.
- Why this explanation is more satisfying: It explains Priya's missed arrival, Tess's sudden readiness, the cropped story, and why the public recap felt coordinated.

## 5. Transformation Types Used

- Selective cropping: The empty-chair story hides the flyer edit, venue-change note, and Tess stepping into the slot.
- Temporal inversion: Public posts imply Priya ignored the event first, but Tess redirected the venue information before Priya went silent.
- Public/private mismatch: Frame and Threadline posts say "no reply," while Blink shows Priya asking for the room number in the wrong thread.
- Role reversal: Tess appears to save the showcase but actually benefited from creating the gap.
- Context collapse: A logistical miss becomes a moral judgment when the feed sees only the empty chair and caption.

## 6. App Semantic Roles

### Frame

- Role in scenario: Creates the first social judgment through visual embarrassment.
- Key artifact: Empty chair carousel with Priya's name card and Noor's caption.
- Misleading surface: Priya looks absent and inconsiderate.
- Truth it reveals: Cropping and later story order show the chair post was framed after Tess took the slot.
- Unlock trigger: Comments ask whether anyone saw the Loop clip of Tess filling in.
- Adversarial concern: The empty chair could feel too on-the-nose if not grounded in realistic community posts.

### Loop

- Role in scenario: Shows the awkward live moment and Tess's public performance.
- Key artifact: Short clip of Tess saying "I can jump in if we need to keep moving."
- Misleading surface: Tess looks helpful and prepared.
- Truth it reveals: Tess had slides and print samples ready before the supposed scramble.
- Unlock trigger: A Loop comment links to a Threadline recap thread about "creative scene etiquette."
- Adversarial concern: Tess's readiness must be suspicious but not instantly conclusive.

### Threadline

- Role in scenario: Shows how the narrative becomes a status lesson and who seeded the wording.
- Key artifact: Recap thread with cropped screenshots and "flyer over room" phrasing.
- Misleading surface: The recap reads like community accountability.
- Truth it reveals: The recap uses phrasing from Tess's old Blink message and omits the venue-change detail.
- Unlock trigger: A reply posts a blurred Blink screenshot showing two location links.
- Adversarial concern: Public discourse must not become a wall of text.

### Blink

- Role in scenario: Reveals private logistics and the missing update path.
- Key artifact: Group chat excerpt with Priya asking "new room or same address?" after Tess forwarded the old link.
- Misleading surface: Priya looks confused and late.
- Truth it reveals: Priya was working from outdated information while Tess was in the final update thread.
- Unlock trigger: Threadline reply references "the group chat receipts."
- Adversarial concern: The private chat should clarify timing without feeling like a direct reveal handed to the player.

## 7. Progressive App Disclosure

- Starting app and why: Frame starts with a visual, emotionally legible empty-chair story.
- Unlock 2 trigger: Frame comments point to the Loop clip of Tess stepping in.
- Unlock 3 trigger: Loop comments point to a Threadline recap that shaped the public judgment.
- Unlock 4 trigger: Threadline replies reference Blink screenshots with the venue links.
- What question each unlock answers: Loop answers how Tess became visible; Threadline answers how the story spread; Blink answers whether Priya had the right information.
- What new question each unlock creates: Loop asks why Tess was prepared; Threadline asks who seeded the wording; Blink asks who controlled the update path.

## 9. Contradiction Ladder

- Surface belief: Priya chased status and skipped a community showcase.
- First wobble: Tess appears too prepared in the Loop clip for a last-minute fill-in.
- Cross-app context: Threadline recap repeats language Tess used earlier and omits venue confusion.
- Timeline break: Frame story order shows Tess's replacement moment came before Noor's sharp caption.
- Source/method link: Blink DMs show Tess sent the old location and Priya was absent from the final update thread.
- Motive/benefit: Tess gained the featured slot, polished public praise, and post-event visibility.
- Confirmation bundle: STAT-02, STAT-04, STAT-06, STAT-08, STAT-09, and STAT-10 make the sabotage path coherent.

## 10. Report Requirements

- Correct WHO: Tess Malik.
- Correct WHY: She wanted Priya's featured slot and the status that came with filling it.
- Correct WHEN: Between the venue change and the first public empty-chair posts.
- Correct HOW: She kept Priya on outdated location info, stepped into the slot, and amplified a cropped public story.
- Minimum required receipts: STAT-02, STAT-04, STAT-06, STAT-08, STAT-09, STAT-10.
- Optional supporting receipts: STAT-03, STAT-05, STAT-07, STAT-11, STAT-12.
- Accepted substitute receipt bundles: STAT-07 plus STAT-11 can support WHEN if STAT-10 is missing, but STAT-08 and STAT-09 remain required for HOW.

## 11. Plausible Wrong Theories

### Priya skipped because she got a better invite

- Why plausible: She missed the showcase, and the empty chair post is emotionally convincing.
- What it explains: The public embarrassment and frustration from the room.
- What it fails to explain: Priya's old-location check-in and Tess's outdated address message.
- Social-native feedback: "This matches the empty chair, but it does not explain why Priya was at the old room asking for the update."

### Noor publicly set Priya up to protect the event

- Why plausible: Noor posted the pointed caption and controlled the event page.
- What it explains: The sharp Frame story and updated flyer.
- What it fails to explain: Tess's private address message and unusually prepared Loop clip.
- Social-native feedback: "Noor shaped the caption, but the location gap started before Noor posted."

### The venue change was just messy and nobody meant harm

- Why plausible: Small events often have chaotic logistics.
- What it explains: The two links and Priya's confusion.
- What it fails to explain: Tess forwarding the old address, having a polished talk ready, and seeding the same moral phrasing.
- Social-native feedback: "The event was messy, but the same person benefits from every messy part."

## 12. Ending Semantics

- Public story: Priya cared more about being listed than showing up for the community.
- True story: Tess redirected Priya's information path, stepped into the gap, and let a cropped public moment turn logistics into status judgment.
- Emotional payoff: The player sees the difference between a messy absence and a socially engineered reputation hit.
- Why the false story spread: The empty chair was visually simple, the caption gave people a moral angle, and Tess looked like the person who saved the day.
- How the feed reacts after the report: Priya posts a low-drama correction with her location receipts, while the recap thread gets quote-posted for leaving out the route change.

## 14. Production Feasibility Notes

- Realistic photos needed: 5-7, including event room, empty chair, flyer, old venue hallway, Tess/Priya profile images.
- Short videos needed: 1-2, mainly Tess stepping into the slot; can be produced as a short controlled clip or still-sequence placeholder.
- Comments/text content needed: 25-35 comments/posts across Frame, Loop, and Threadline, plus 2-3 Blink snippets.
- Risky assets: Event flyer must look fictional; venue names must avoid real places; public callout tone must stay contained.
- Assets that can be made from stills: Empty chair, flyer updates, venue hallway, Threadline recap, Blink DMs.
- Assets that require careful continuity: Timestamps, room names, Priya's old-location check-in, Tess's prepared slides.

## 15. Self-Adversarial Critique

- What is too obvious: Tess's readiness in the Loop clip could make her the answer too early if the clip overplays the prepared slides.
- What is too hidden: The missing update path could be confusing if the two venue links are not visually distinct.
- What app feels unnecessary: Loop could feel like a duplicate of Frame unless Tess's public performance carries real status meaning.
- What receipt feels too confession-like: STAT-08 must be framed as a normal forwarded address, not a clear admission of sabotage.
- Where players might get stuck: Understanding who was in which Blink thread could be hard on a small screen.
- What feels unrealistic: A small event public pile-on can feel exaggerated unless the community status stakes are established early.
- What should be changed before playtesting: Make the community feel real through atmospheric posts, keep Tess sympathetic at first, and ensure Priya has enough public presence to avoid feeling like a flat victim of the feed.
