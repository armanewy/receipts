## Critic Perspectives

### 1. Mystery Structure Critic

- Score: 4.2 / 5
- Public interpretation plausibility: Strong. The empty chair plus pointed caption gives the feed a clean moral read.
- Actual causal chain explanatory power: Strong. Tess's outdated link, Priya's old-location behavior, and Tess's replacement benefit explain the public moment better than "Priya bailed."
- Contradiction ladder strength: Strong and easy to follow.
- Single-receipt dependency: Low to moderate. STAT-08 is important, but STAT-02, STAT-04, STAT-09, and STAT-10 all contribute meaningfully.
- Responsible person visibility: Tess appears early enough and has a public role that can shift from helpful to suspicious.
- Wrong theories: Good. Noor and general venue chaos are especially valuable because they keep the public story from collapsing too early.
- Brute-force risk: Moderate. Tess may be easy to pick if her preparedness is overplayed.

### 2. Doomscroll/UX Critic

- Score: 4.2 / 5
- First 30-second pull: Strong. A story carousel with an empty chair and pointed caption is a clean mobile hook.
- App unlock momentum: Strong. Frame comments to Loop, Loop comments to Threadline, and Threadline replies to Blink all feel natural.
- "One more thing" motivation: Strong. The player will likely want to know whether Priya was absent, late, or misdirected.
- Browsing versus puzzle feel: Strong if venue-link comparison is visual and conversational rather than presented as a matching task.
- Report screen interruption: Likely low. The theory/report slots map naturally to social questions players already have.
- App desire: Frame and Blink are essential. Loop becomes essential only if Tess's public performance materially changes her status.

### 3. Social Realism Critic

- Score: 4.0 / 5
- Would people post this way: Yes, especially in a small creator/workshop environment where status and attendance become public signals.
- Internet-native drama: Strong. A cropped story, moralizing recap thread, and private logistics screenshot fit the product.
- Receipt believability: Strong, as long as Blink excerpts feel leaked/saved rather than omniscient.
- Scripted/artificial risk: Moderate. Tess having slides, phrasing, an old link, and replacement branding can feel too arranged.
- Emotional believability: Strong. Resentment over a featured slot is specific and human-scale.

### 4. Production Feasibility Critic

- Score: 4.6 / 5
- Photo/video asset burden: Low to medium. Most important artifacts can be stills, screenshots, and text.
- Continuity challenges: Manageable. Room names, story order, and timestamps matter, but no tiny visual details need to carry the answer.
- Unique people/locations: Reasonable.
- Video precision: Low. Tess's fill-in clip can be a short controlled clip or a still-sequence placeholder.
- Text/stills potential: Excellent.
- IP sensitivity: Low if flyer, app surfaces, and venue names are fictional.

### 5. Ethical/Safety Critic

- Score: 4.0 / 5
- Real-person/event risk: Low with fictional names, venues, and community details.
- Exploitative feel: Manageable. The scenario should avoid inviting the player to enjoy Priya's humiliation too much.
- Harassment-like behavior risk: Moderate because the surface event is a public callout. The ending should correct the story without escalating a pile-on against Tess.
- Fictional contained lurking: Strong. The player browses authored content and saves receipts.
- Ending safety: Needs restraint. Priya's correction should be low-drama and context-focused.

### 6. Product Fit Critic

- Score: 4.3 / 5
- North star match: Strong. This feels like falling down a social feed and realizing the public story is missing context.
- Vocabulary fit: Strong. Future packets should continue using receipts, theory/report, posts, stories, clips, DMs, and source language.
- Internet drama fit: Strong. It is not crime-first, supernatural, or platform-only.
- Four-app use: Good. Frame and Blink are core; Threadline shapes public meaning; Loop must emphasize status transfer.
- Repeatability risk: Low to medium. The empty-chair visual and workshop politics give it a memorable shape.

## Top 5 Strengths

1. The hook is visual, fast, and mobile-friendly.
2. The report requirements are concrete and easy to validate through receipts.
3. The true story is more explanatory than the public interpretation without becoming oversized.
4. Production can rely on manageable stills, screenshots, text posts, and one simple clip.
5. Wrong theories are socially plausible and true-but-incomplete.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. Tess may become too obvious if her prepared talk is too polished too soon.
2. Blink participant lists and venue paths could confuse players on a small screen.
3. Loop may feel redundant unless Tess's fill-in moment creates real status movement.
4. The small community stakes need early texture so the public reaction feels earned.
5. The repeated phrasing trail could feel writerly if it appears too cleanly across apps.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Establish the workshop/community stakes before the empty-chair judgment fully lands: why the slot matters, who follows this scene, and why the room cares.
- Keep Tess sympathetic in the first half. She should look prepared and helpful, not instantly exposed as strategic.
- Make Priya visible as a real person before she is reduced to the empty chair. Give her profile or prior post enough warmth to complicate the pile-on.
- Make Loop's role more than "Tess was prepared." It should show the status transfer: audience praise, new followers, or a clipped moment that changes her social position.
- Use room names or venue labels that are readable at a glance; do not make the player compare tiny URLs.
- Make Noor's caption emotionally understandable but incomplete, not malicious.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut or soften STAT-03 if the updated flyer makes Tess's preparation feel too blatant.
- Cut one repeated phrase connection if the "flyer over room" trail feels too neatly authored.
- Cut Threadline length aggressively; keep the recap finite and scannable.
- Cut any private-message line that makes Tess's intent explicit.
- Cut any extra venue logistics that do not support WHEN or HOW.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Show the venue change as two distinct, human-readable destinations.
- Make the Blink group membership visually clear: who was in the final update thread, who was not, and who had access.
- Clarify the story order: Tess fills in, the empty-chair caption spreads, then the recap moralizes it.
- Make Priya's old-location check-in broad and readable without depending on tiny background signs.
- Define the featured slot's value through social reaction, not exposition.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Let Tess's resentment surface through small competitive details rather than a direct hostile line.
- Make the old-address forward look plausibly helpful on first read.
- Let Noor's public post be a heat-of-the-moment reaction to incomplete context.
- Give Priya at least one small flaw so the public interpretation remains plausible.
- Make the public recap account opportunistic, not simply Tess's puppet.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium. Tess is the replacement, has the polished clip, uses repeated phrasing, and sends the old address. If all of this appears early, the player may choose Tess before the receipt graph has room to breathe. Delay or soften at least one of those signals.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Low to medium. The core is legible, but Blink thread membership could be messy. The UI/content plan should use clear participant chips, simple timestamps, and distinct room labels.

## Production Risk

Low to medium. This is one of the most feasible candidates. The highest-risk pieces are the Tess fill-in clip, readable venue/timestamp continuity, and making the public reaction feel big enough without requiring too many unique posts.

## Wave 4 Recommendation

Move to Wave 4 as a top candidate. It has a strong hook, clear receipt requirements, and a feasible content burden. Wave 4 should focus on making Tess less obvious, giving the community more texture, and ensuring Loop has a distinct status-transfer purpose.
