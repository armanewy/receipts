# Semantic Scenario Bible

## 1. Core Product Premise

Receipts is a solo mobile-first lurker mystery experience. The player scrolls fictional social apps, saves receipts, compares context, and assembles a theory/report. The player is a lurker, not a professional problem-solver. Browsing is the fantasy; mystery-solving is the mechanic.

Receipts are social artifacts, not forensic evidence. A clip, comment, story, DM, profile edit, repost, quote, or screenshot matters because its meaning changes as the player sees more of the social graph.

Product-facing language should use receipts, posts, clips, stories, DMs, comments, profiles, sources, threads, theories, reports, exposes, WHO, WHY, WHEN, and HOW. Avoid detective, police, case-file, suspect, culprit, perp, investigation, and crime-scene vocabulary in product-facing design.

## 2. Scenario As A Semantic Object

A scenario is a proof-shaped social drama. It is not a prose prompt or a sentence template. Each scenario must define the following layers:

- Surface Event: the public artifact or moment that makes players care.
- Public Interpretation: the low-effort story the feed encourages.
- Actual Causal Chain: what really happened and why it explains more.
- Transformation Types: meaning shifts that turn receipts from surface signals into deeper context.
- App Semantic Roles: why Loop, Frame, Threadline, and Blink each matter.
- Receipt Graph: linked artifacts with apparent meaning, actual meaning, dependencies, and report relevance.
- Contradiction Ladder: staged moments where the surface story starts to wobble.
- Report Requirements: the claims and receipt bundle required for WHO, WHY, WHEN, HOW, and RECEIPTS.
- Wrong Theories: plausible but incomplete readings.
- Ending Semantics: what changes after the report and why the false story spread.
- Adversarial Review: a serious critique of clarity, realism, production, safety, and product fit.

## 3. Surface Event Semantics

A surface event is the social spark. It should have:

- Immediate social salience: a player can understand why people are reacting.
- Emotional legibility: embarrassment, betrayal, hypocrisy, jealousy, disappointment, betrayal, status loss, or public awkwardness.
- Shareability: the event can plausibly circulate as a clip, screenshot, quote, story, or thread.
- Ambiguity under the surface: the public story feels strong but incomplete.
- A reason to scroll deeper: replies, tags, missing context, weird timing, or a named account invite more tapping.

The surface event should not solve itself. It should create judgment first, then doubt.

## 4. Public Interpretation Semantics

The public interpretation is the story the feed wants the player to accept quickly.

It must define:

- Apparent responsible person.
- Apparent motive.
- Apparent timeline.
- Apparent wronged party.
- Artifact that makes it feel true.
- Why it spreads socially.
- What it gets right.
- What it gets wrong.

The public interpretation should be emotionally sticky. It should reward the player's first judgment while leaving enough friction for later receipts to complicate it.

## 5. Actual Causal Chain Semantics

The actual causal chain is the hidden sequence that makes the full receipt graph cohere.

It must define:

- Responsible individual.
- Motive or pressure.
- Action taken.
- Manipulation or concealment method.
- Real timeline.
- Consequence.
- Trace left behind.

The true chain must explain more than the public interpretation. It should make earlier receipts feel recontextualized rather than simply wrong.

## 6. Transformation Types

Use transformation types to describe how meaning changes.

- Temporal inversion: a post presented as a cause was actually a reaction, or old content is treated as new.
- Context collapse: a post meant for one audience is judged by another without the missing frame.
- Source laundering: a weak or biased claim gains credibility through reposts, quote threads, or fan accounts.
- Selective cropping: an image, clip, quote, or screenshot hides the surrounding content that changes its meaning.
- Role reversal: the apparent harmed person had more agency than the feed suggests, or the apparent aggressor was responding to pressure.
- Soft-launch contradiction: relationship hints, background details, tags, or timing conflict with the public romantic timeline.
- Alt-account leakage: language patterns, posting cadence, objects, contacts, or repeated details connect an account to a person.
- Incentive trail: visible benefits, opportunities, or status gains explain why someone shaped the story.
- Public/private mismatch: a public statement conflicts with private messages or ephemeral posts.
- Timestamp contradiction: post times, story order, edit times, or event times break the public timeline.
- Old-content reframing: older posts are resurfaced to support a new narrative they were not originally about.
- Amplifier/source separation: the loudest account is not the origin, and the origin tells a different story.

## 7. Receipt Semantics

Each receipt must be modeled as:

- Source app.
- Surface type.
- Raw artifact.
- Apparent meaning.
- Actual meaning.
- Report relevance.
- Required, optional, or misleading.
- Dependencies.
- Confidence contribution.

Required receipts support report slots. Optional receipts add confidence, tone, or alternate paths. Misleading receipts should still be true artifacts whose meaning changes with context.

## 8. Contradiction Ladder

The contradiction ladder should stage understanding:

1. Surface belief: the public interpretation feels plausible.
2. First wobble: one detail does not quite fit.
3. Cross-app context: a second app changes the frame.
4. Timeline break: the order of events becomes unstable.
5. Source/method link: the player sees how the story was shaped.
6. Motive/benefit: the responsible person's reason becomes legible.
7. Confirmation bundle: multiple receipts support the report.

The ladder should avoid a single magic receipt. Confidence should accumulate.

## 9. Report Semantics

The report is a structured theory surface.

- WHO: the individual whose action shaped the true story.
- WHY: the pressure, desire, benefit, or fear that made the action make sense.
- WHEN: the meaningful timeline window or sequence.
- HOW: the social method used, such as cropping, repost timing, alt leakage, rumor seeding, or private/public mismatch.
- RECEIPTS: saved artifacts that make the theory coherent.

Feedback should sound social-native: "This explains the launch post, but not the late-night repost" rather than procedural or institutional.

## 10. Red Herring Semantics

Red herrings must be true-but-incomplete. They can point to jealousy, bad behavior, awkward timing, reputation pressure, or a misleading source. They should not be arbitrary false clues.

A good wrong theory:

- Explains at least one real receipt.
- Feels emotionally plausible.
- Fails to explain another required receipt.
- Teaches the player what context is missing.

## 11. Doomscroll Engagement Semantics

Emotional beats:

- Curiosity: "Why is everyone reacting?"
- Judgment: "I think I know what happened."
- Doubt: "That detail does not fit."
- Surprise: "This app changes the story."
- Suspicion: "Someone shaped this on purpose."
- Confirmation: "The pieces line up."
- Satisfaction: "The theory explains more than the feed did."

Core loop:

1. Scroll.
2. Notice.
3. Tap deeper.
4. Save receipt.
5. Unlock another app.
6. Compare context.
7. Form theory.
8. Need one more receipt.

The loop works when every answer creates a sharper next question.

## 12. Scenario Approval Checklist

- The player remains a lurker.
- The hook is understandable in the first 30 seconds.
- The surface story is socially plausible.
- The responsible party is an individual.
- The true causal chain explains more than the public story.
- At least three transformation types are used meaningfully.
- All app surfaces earn their role.
- App unlocks are motivated by content.
- The receipt graph requires a bundle, not one decisive artifact.
- Wrong theories are plausible but incomplete.
- The tone is internet drama, not horror or procedural.
- Real platform names, brands, public figures, and lookalikes are avoided.
- Critical receipts do not depend on tiny visual pixels.
- The scenario includes adversarial review.
