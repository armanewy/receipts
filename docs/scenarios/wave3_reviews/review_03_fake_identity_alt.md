## Mystery Structure Critic

- Perspective score: 4.0 / 5
- Public interpretation plausibility: Strong. A green hoodie avatar, Leah-coded phrasing, and an annoyed lunch carousel create an emotionally simple reading that people would repeat quickly.
- Actual causal chain explanatory power: Strong. Noah explains the private-context access, the copied phrasing, the account timing, and the benefit from Leah losing trust.
- Contradiction ladder strength: Good escalation. The first wobble is timeline-based, then Frame supplies physical access, Blink supplies private timing, and Threadline/Loop tie source and method together.
- Single-receipt dependency: Moderate risk. R03-10, the export draft with `NR_export_roomtemp_v2`, may feel like it directly names the answer if too readable or too unique.
- Responsible person appears early enough: Yes. Noah appears in Frame and Blink before the final bundle, but he should be visible in low-stakes social context earlier so the answer does not feel imported late.
- Wrong theories: Leah and Mara are strong. The random fan-account theory is useful but should be sharpened with a reason the fan account looks especially credible.
- Report brute-force risk: Manageable. The report needs WHO, WHY, WHEN, and HOW, but Noah's motive and method may become obvious if R03-05 and R03-10 appear too close together.

## Doomscroll/UX Critic

- Perspective score: 4.1 / 5
- First 30-second pull: Strong. The side-by-side hoodie read is visual, fast, and emotionally legible.
- App unlock momentum: Strong. Loop points to Threadline, Threadline points to Frame, and Frame/Threadline point to Blink through a private nickname.
- One-more-thing motivation: Strongest around "why did the account exist before Leah had a reason?" and "why was Noah wearing the hoodie?"
- Browsing feel: Mostly preserved. The player is comparing posts, comments, stories, and DMs rather than solving a formal puzzle.
- Report screen fit: The report should emphasize "which reading fits the receipts" rather than a formal accusation. Feedback should stay casual and feed-native.
- App desire: Threadline has a clear source-trail purpose, Frame has access and social context, Blink has timing and motive. Loop must keep emotion alive after the first clip rather than becoming only the doorway.

## Social Realism Critic

- Perspective score: 4.0 / 5
- Posting behavior: Plausible. Creator-house anxiety, borrowed clothing, editing roles, and recap accounts are recognizable internet-drama material.
- Drama texture: Strong. The quiet-friend-blamed-for-alt-account hook feels native to creator circles and fandom discourse.
- Receipt believability: Mostly strong. Language-pattern comparison is believable if it is not treated as conclusive alone.
- Scripted/artificial risk: The export-folder name is the main artificial note. It should look like a mundane workflow artifact, not a label designed for the player.
- Emotional believability: Noah's motive is legible without being enormous: credit, income, and status inside a group.

## Production Feasibility Critic

- Perspective score: 4.0 / 5
- Photo/video burden: Manageable. The scenario needs a few stills, one main reaction clip, and screenshots.
- Continuity challenges: The hoodie, watch, edit-room chair, and timestamps are the key continuity objects. They need to be readable at mobile size without relying on tiny details.
- Cast/location count: Moderate and acceptable: Leah, Noah, Mara, and a few group members, mostly in studio/lunch contexts.
- Video precision: Low to moderate. The Loop clip can be built from still-heavy vertical video and UI overlays.
- Stills/text viability: High. Threadline and Blink can carry much of the causal chain.
- IP/App Store sensitivity: Low if the fictional apps avoid real marks and the alt-account harassment energy is kept contained.

## Ethical/Safety Critic

- Perspective score: 4.0 / 5
- Real-person resemblance risk: Low if cast generation uses fictional references and avoids public-figure styling.
- Exploitative tone risk: Moderate. A public pile-on around a falsely blamed person can feel harsh if the ending invites vindictive posting.
- Harassment-like behavior: Needs containment. The product should frame saving receipts as in-world browsing, not as instruction for real-life targeting.
- Fictional lurking boundary: Preserved, assuming Blink content is authored/unlocked through scenario surfaces rather than implying invasive access.
- Ending tone: Should not become "go after Noah." It should focus on how the feed misread Leah and how the receipts reframe the story.

## Product Fit Critic

- Perspective score: 4.1 / 5
- North star match: Strong. The scenario is browsing-first, cross-app, and built around semantic recontextualization.
- Vocabulary discipline: Good. The draft uses receipts, theory/report, posts, comments, clips, and app surfaces. Keep future revisions away from restricted product terms.
- Internet drama fit: Strong. Fake alt-account blame and creator-house role anxiety fit the product well.
- App archetype usage: Strong. All four apps earn their place.
- Interchangeability risk: Moderate. The "person frames another person using a cropped social artifact" pattern could recur often; the hoodie/edit-role specifics help differentiate it.

## Top 5 Strengths

1. The hoodie/avatar surface hook is immediately readable and mobile-friendly.
2. Noah's motive is socially believable and individual-centered.
3. Each app reveals a different layer: spread, source, access, private timing.
4. Wrong theories are plausible without being arbitrary.
5. The receipt graph has a clear route from first judgment to revised theory.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. R03-10 may be too convenient and answer-shaped.
2. The punctuation/language-pattern receipts may be too subtle unless paired with stronger surrounding context.
3. Noah could feel under-seeded if he appears mainly when the scenario needs a new answer.
4. The fan recap account needs a more specific social role to feel real rather than functional.
5. Leah's apparent motive may not be strong enough after the timeline wobble starts.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Rework R03-10 so it supports Noah's workflow connection without directly tying initials, handle, and account name in one artifact.
- Add one earlier low-stakes Noah presence on Loop or Frame so players remember him before the answer path sharpens.
- Give the fan recap account a defined identity, such as a known group-update account with a history of fast reposts.
- Strengthen Leah's surface motive with a receipt that is true but incomplete, such as a vague comment about being left out of a shoot schedule.
- Make the language-pattern comparison one part of a bundle, not a standalone WHO answer.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut or soften the exact `NR_export_roomtemp_v2` wording. It is too tidy.
- Cut any receipt that asks players to identify tiny watch/chair details from a small crop.
- Cut repeated explanations that the recap account "verified" the story; one clear timestamped amplification path is enough.
- Cut any Blink wording that sounds like Noah explaining his own plan.
- Cut any extra group members who do not affect the receipt graph.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Clarify the true order: alt creation, Noah hearing role news, escalation, Leah public frustration.
- Clarify why Leah's "tiny chaos" phrase is publicly available to Noah through edited captions.
- Clarify why Mara's Loop clip is an amplifier, not the origin.
- Clarify how the Blink group-chat nickname unlocks the private context in-world.
- Clarify which receipts are required for WHO versus HOW so players do not treat hoodie access as the whole answer.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Make Noah's motive feel anxious and self-protective, not cartoonishly malicious.
- Let Leah have enough real frustration that the public read remains tempting.
- Make the hoodie borrowing casual and socially normal before it becomes meaningful.
- Let the fan recap account sound like an overeager community account, not a planted hint.
- Use repeated phrasing sparingly so the player notices a pattern without feeling pointed at.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium. If Frame shows Noah in the hoodie and Loop shows an export draft with his initials and the alt handle, players may reach the right WHO and HOW too quickly. Spread these receipts apart and make each one incomplete alone.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Medium. If the answer leans too much on slash spacing, cadence, and timestamp comparison, players may miss the intended pattern. Pair subtle language receipts with clear motive and access receipts.

## Production Risk

Moderate. Hoodie continuity is easy to produce, but avatar cropping and mobile readability need careful art direction. The scenario should avoid relying on tiny accessories or background props as required report support.

## Should Move To Wave 4?

Yes, with revisions. This is a strong candidate for development because the hook, app roles, and receipt graph match the product. Wave 4 should primarily tune R03-10, deepen Noah's early presence, and strengthen Leah's tempting-but-incomplete surface read.
