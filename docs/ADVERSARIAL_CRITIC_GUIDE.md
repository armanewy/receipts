# Adversarial Critic Guide

The critic's job is to stress-test scenarios before production. The critic should be direct, concrete, and specific about cuts, clarifications, and subtlety improvements.

## Required Questions

- Is this just fake drama or a true mystery?
- Is the public interpretation emotionally sticky?
- Is the actual causal chain more explanatory?
- Does each app earn its place?
- Are receipts realistic and cross-app?
- Is the answer too confession-like?
- Is the solution too dependent on one receipt?
- Can users brute-force the report?
- Are wrong theories plausible but incomplete?
- Is the tone internet drama, not horror?
- Does it avoid detective/police language?
- Does it preserve doomscroll behavior?
- Is production feasible with realistic AI-generated media?
- What should be cut, clarified, or made subtler?

## Review Dimensions

### Mystery Structure

- Public interpretation plausibility.
- Actual causal chain explanatory power.
- Contradiction ladder strength.
- Receipt graph depth.
- Wrong theory quality.
- Report clarity.

### Doomscroll/UX

- First 30-second pull.
- One-more-tap momentum.
- App unlock motivation.
- Whether browsing remains the core fantasy.
- Whether report assembly feels earned.

### Social Realism

- Would people post this way?
- Does the drama feel internet-native?
- Are comments varied and believable?
- Does the true story fit human motives?
- Does the public story spread for a clear social reason?

### Production Feasibility

- Realistic photo/video burden.
- Continuity difficulty.
- Unique cast and location count.
- Whether text/stills can carry enough of the experience.
- Which receipts create production risk.

### Ethical And Safety Fit

- No real people, public figures, lookalikes, or real platforms.
- No real-world harassment instructions or encouragement.
- Lurking remains fictional and contained.
- Ending avoids real-world callout vibes.
- Sensitive topics are handled with restraint.

### Product Fit

- Preserves lurker framing.
- Avoids product-facing detective/law-enforcement language.
- Uses receipts, theory, report, and social artifacts.
- Fits broad internet drama.
- Avoids becoming an interchangeable formula.

## Output Expectations

A critic review should include:

- Overall verdict: KEEP, REVISE, or DISCARD.
- Numeric scores using the scenario rubric.
- Top strengths.
- Top weaknesses.
- Specific revision recommendations.
- Specific cuts.
- Specific clarity improvements.
- Specific subtlety improvements.
- Risk of being too easy.
- Risk of being too hard.
- Production risk.
- Recommendation on whether to move forward.
