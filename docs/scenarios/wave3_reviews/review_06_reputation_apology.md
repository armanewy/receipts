## 1. Mystery Structure Critic

- Perspective score: 4.2 / 5
- Public interpretation plausibility: Excellent. Mina's own apology and the cropped planning chat make the surface story feel nearly self-confirming.
- Actual causal chain explanatory power: Strong. Sloane explains why the apology posted early, why the crop spread, and who benefited from the seat opening.
- Contradiction ladder strength: Strong and easy to follow: apology timing, Talia's later response, Sloane's early badge, then Blink pressure.
- Solution dependence on one receipt: Mostly avoided. R06-10 is the biggest risk because forwarding a crop to "TTN" can over-solve HOW and WHO if too explicit.
- Responsible person timing: Sloane appears early through replies and Frame, which is good. She should initially read as a supportive friend, not an obvious operator.
- Wrong theories: Very strong. Mina is meaningfully responsible for messy hosting even if she is not the person who shaped the public wave.
- Brute-force risk: Moderate-low. The answer needs a bundle across timing, source path, benefit, and crop context.

## 2. Doomscroll/UX Critic

- Perspective score: 4.1 / 5
- First 30-second pull: Strong. Public apology waves are familiar, fast, and emotionally legible.
- App unlock momentum: Strong. Replies naturally point to Loop, Loop naturally points to Sloane's Frame story, and Frame gives a believable Blink preview.
- One-more-thing motivation: Strong. The player keeps asking why Mina posted early, who supplied the crop, and why Sloane already had the seat.
- Browsing feel: Strong. The scenario feels like tapping quotes, checking timestamps, opening stories, and saving screenshots.
- Report screen risk: Low. WHO / WHY / WHEN / HOW map cleanly to a social theory.
- App desire: Threadline and Frame are compelling. Loop should add social texture, not only a timeline comparison.

## 3. Social Realism Critic

- Perspective score: 4 / 5
- Posting behavior: Believable. Creators do post rushed apologies, friends do shape narratives, and commentary accounts do flatten context.
- Internet-native feel: Strong. The dinner-seat and sponsor-access dynamics are recognizable without needing real-world references.
- Receipt believability: Strong. Cropped chats, close-friends stories, reply timing, and quote threads are credible surfaces.
- Scripted-feeling risk: Medium-low. The "earned the seat quietly" caption is a little too on the nose and should become more casual.
- Emotional believability: Good. Mina's panic and Sloane's status pressure both make sense, but Mina needs enough vulnerability to explain why she posts before checking with Talia.

## 4. Production Feasibility Critic

- Perspective score: 4 / 5
- Asset burden: Manageable. The scenario can work with text posts, story screenshots, a place card, and one screen-record-style Loop clip.
- Continuity challenges: Moderate but controllable: timestamps, crop boundaries, guest-list names, and Sloane's seat timeline must line up.
- Unique people/locations: Moderate. Mina, Talia, Sloane, a commentary account, and a dinner context are enough.
- Video precision: Low. Loop can be represented as a reaction clip with screen captures and voice/caption overlays.
- Lower-cost assets: Threadline posts, Blink messages, and Frame story screenshots carry most of the key meaning.
- High-risk assets: Sponsor badge and dinner branding must be fictional and visually generic.

## 5. Ethical/Safety Critic

- Perspective score: 4 / 5
- Real-person/event imitation: Manageable if the creator dinner, sponsor, and cast are kept fictional and not modeled on a current controversy.
- Exploitative feel: Low-to-moderate. The drama is social-status oriented rather than trauma-driven.
- Harassment-like energy: Manageable. The ending should avoid making the player feel like they are launching a public pile-on.
- Fictional containment: Strong with fictional app names and fictional creator brands.
- Ending safety: Good if it preserves nuance: Mina mishandled the situation, but Sloane shaped the public story for personal gain.

## 6. Product Fit Critic

- Perspective score: 4.3 / 5
- North star match: Strong. The player browses, saves receipts, compares app contexts, and assembles a theory.
- Vocabulary fit: Strong. The scenario can be framed entirely through receipts, posts, stories, DMs, and report slots.
- Internet drama fit: Excellent. Public apology, callout timing, cropped chats, and creator access all fit the product's lane.
- Four-app use: Strong. Threadline starts the public wave, Loop makes the time mismatch legible, Frame shows status benefit, Blink reveals private pressure.
- Repeatability risk: Medium-low. This has a recognizable social pattern, but the dinner-seat/sponsor shape gives it enough specificity.

## Top 5 Strengths

1. The public interpretation is highly believable because Mina's apology reads like confirmation.
2. The timing contradiction is easy to understand without relying on tiny visual details.
3. Sloane's motive is concrete: seat access and sponsor proximity.
4. Wrong theories are strong because Mina, Talia, and the commentary account each explain part of the receipt graph.
5. The scenario is production-friendly, leaning on text, stories, and controlled screen-record-style media.

## Top 5 Weaknesses

1. R06-10 could become too direct if the forwarded crop clearly names the commentary account.
2. Sloane's "earned the seat quietly" caption is too revealing.
3. Loop could feel like a utility surface rather than a social app if it only compares timestamps.
4. Mina posting early needs stronger emotional setup to feel realistic.
5. The sponsor-linked series opportunity needs just enough context to matter without becoming exposition-heavy.

## Specific Revision Recommendations

- Keep Mina partly accountable for messy hosting, but make Sloane the person who shaped the feed story.
- Revise Sloane's Frame caption to sound casual and status-seeking, not too revealing.
- Make R06-10 a partial forwarding path with initials, a cropped contact preview, or a reply reaction rather than a named handoff.
- Add one or two Loop comments that point to Sloane through social familiarity, not hint-like phrasing.
- Give Mina a private reason to panic before noon, such as sponsor pressure or fear of looking dismissive, while keeping Sloane's nudge central.

## Specific Cuts

- Cut any Blink line where Sloane says she wants Talia's seat outright.
- Cut any Threadline reply that tells the player exactly which app to check in instructional language.
- Cut overly neat wording around "earned the seat quietly."
- Cut redundant apology quote-posts if they restate the same Mina blame without changing timing or source context.
- Cut any sponsor asset that resembles a real brand campaign.

## Specific Clarity Improvements

- Clarify what Table Draft is and why the dinner matters socially within the first few artifacts.
- Clarify why the after-dinner series test makes the seat valuable.
- Clarify whether Talia was definitely removed or conditionally swapped because of travel uncertainty.
- Clarify the exact order: Sloane badge, Mina apology, commentary thread, Talia reply.
- Clarify how the cropped planning chat reached public discourse without making it a direct one-message answer.

## Specific Subtlety Improvements

- Make Sloane's early behavior supportive enough that players can plausibly trust her.
- Let the sponsor incentive appear first as atmosphere, then become motive after the receipt bundle grows.
- Make the cropped chat emotionally persuasive, not obviously cropped at first glance.
- Give Mina's apology language enough awkwardness that it looks confirming on first read.
- Let Talia's later response be confused and boundary-setting rather than neatly explanatory.

## Risk Of Being Too Easy

Medium-low. If Sloane's Frame story and Blink forward both point directly to her, players may identify WHO too quickly. Keep her benefit visible before her method is clear, and make the report require the timestamp and crop-context receipts.

## Risk Of Being Too Hard

Low-to-medium. The central timestamp contradiction is readable. The hardest part will be making players understand that a public apology can happen before the story it appears to answer. The Loop reaction should make that concept clear without solving the whole scenario.

## Production Risk

Low-to-moderate. This is one of the more feasible candidates. Main risks are maintaining timestamp consistency, making crop boundaries readable, and ensuring the fictional sponsor/dinner assets do not evoke real brands or current events.

## Wave 4 Recommendation

Move to Wave 4. Develop this as a top candidate unless another scenario has a much stronger first 30 seconds. The Wave 4 packet should focus on subtle Sloane presentation, timestamp clarity, and keeping Loop socially textured.
