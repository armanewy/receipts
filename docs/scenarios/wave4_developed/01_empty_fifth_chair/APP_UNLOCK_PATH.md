# The Empty Fifth Chair: App Unlock Path

## Starting App And Why

Start on Frame. The empty fifth chair is the most instantly legible visual surface and carries the friend-group status wound. Frame also lets the player see public curation before they understand the timing: old highlight names, group photos, story captions, tags, and bio edits.

The opening should feel like browsing a social profile during a public friend fallout, not like receiving instructions.

## Unlock 1: Frame To Loop

### Exact Semantic Trigger

Mina's Frame story includes a repost sticker for Dax's Loop clip, framed as "you all saw the table clip." Comments on the Frame story also mention Rhea "walking out mid-toast."

### What The Player Has Likely Learned

- Rhea is the missing fifth friend.
- Mina is presenting the exclusion as a response to private screenshots.
- The old group identity was "Supper Five."

### What Question Motivates The Unlock

What happened at the dinner table, and did Rhea really leave because the leak had just surfaced?

### What Loop Reveals

Loop reveals the table clip, the repost chain, and comment friction around when the dinner happened. It makes the public story feel embodied, then destabilizes the order of events.

### In-World Availability Language

- "Dax's table clip is everywhere."
- "People are quoting the Loop from Mina's story."
- "The comments keep asking whether the dinner was actually Tuesday."

## Unlock 2: Loop To Threadline

### Exact Semantic Trigger

Loop comments repeatedly reference @porchwatch's Threadline screenshot as the reason the group turned on Rhea. A pinned comment says the clip "only makes sense if you saw the porchwatch crop."

### What The Player Has Likely Learned

- The dinner clip is emotionally convincing.
- The repost timing may be out of order.
- The public conversation depends on a private chat screenshot.

### What Question Motivates The Unlock

What screenshot made everyone believe Rhea leaked the chat?

### What Threadline Reveals

Threadline shows the cropped Blink screenshot, the repost chain, the low-effort public interpretation, and the first hints that the source is not simply an outside gossip account.

### In-World Availability Language

- "Threadline is where the screenshot started traveling."
- "@porchwatch has the crop everyone is arguing about."
- "The quote-posts are moving faster than the dinner clip."

## Unlock 3: Threadline To Blink

### Exact Semantic Trigger

The @porchwatch screenshot exposes the Blink group name "Supper Five Planning" and partial contact labels. A reply asks why the crop starts in the middle of the chat, which opens access to related Blink context.

### What The Player Has Likely Learned

- The screenshot is cropped.
- @porchwatch amplified the story but may not be the original source.
- The crop's missing context matters.

### What Question Motivates The Unlock

What did the full private chat say before it became a public screenshot?

### What Blink Reveals

Blink reveals the uncropped chat, Rhea's de-escalating message, Mina's removed lines, the Rhea/Mina credit conversation, and the social pressure that kept other friends quiet.

### In-World Availability Language

- "The saved Blink crop now matches a group thread."
- "Related Blink context appears under 'Supper Five Planning.'"
- "Rhea and Mina's older messages explain why Thursday mattered."

## Return Path: Blink Back To Frame And Threadline

### Exact Semantic Trigger

Rhea's Blink DM mentions Cedar Room on Thursday. The player can then return to Frame and find Mina's Cedar Room announcement and profile update. Returning to Threadline shows @porchwatch locking replies after timing questions spread.

### What The Player Has Likely Learned

- Rhea challenged Mina about shared credit.
- Mina's cropped chat view is plausible but needs outside support.
- The public order of events no longer holds.

### What Question Motivates The Return

What did Mina gain from making Rhea look like the disloyal friend before Thursday?

### What The Return Reveals

Frame provides the benefit trail. Threadline shows the public source wobbling under timeline pressure.

## How To Avoid Tutorial-ish Language

- App movement should be triggered by visible social references: repost stickers, handles, tags, group names, comments, and profile links.
- Avoid labels like "new app unlocked" in scenario-facing copy.
- The player should feel they are following social gravity: people keep mentioning another surface, so tapping there feels natural.
- Each app should answer one question and create a sharper next question.

## App Unlock Quality Risks

- Comment-only unlocks can feel too convenient. Use a mix of repost stickers, visible handles, group names, and profile links.
- Blink should not appear as a late answer dump. Its first reveal should reframe the crop, then send the player back to Frame for benefit and timing support.
- Cedar Room should not appear too early. It should be visible as background status first, then become meaningful after Rhea's DM.
